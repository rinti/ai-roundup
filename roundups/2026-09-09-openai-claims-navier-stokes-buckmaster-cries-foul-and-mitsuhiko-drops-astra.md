# AI Roundup — September 9, 2026

The Navier-Stokes claim ate the entire day. OpenAI says its next-gen model solved a Millennium Prize Problem; the mathematician whose approach it may have followed says they fought dirty. Everything else is aftershock.

## The Navier-Stokes Bombshell

### OpenAI claims the proof

OpenAI [announced](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2097374640582668336) that a next-generation internal model — described as "significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra" — produced a Lean-verified proof that smooth 3D fluid motion governed by the Navier-Stokes equations can develop a singularity in finite time, resolving one of the seven [Millennium Prize Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Prize_Problems). The 165-page proof was generated by roughly 10,000 coordinating agents over 88 hours, producing 2.7 million messages and approximately 130 billion output tokens at an estimated cost north of $40 million ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-openai-reports-navier-stokes)). Greg Brockman [called it](https://x.com/gdb/status/2097377042152972398) "a major milestone for both AI and mathematics" and the entry point to "a new era of artificial general intelligence." Dean Ball [flagged](https://x.com/deanwball/status/2097377129381933335) the buried lede: this was not Astra, but an unreleased model beyond it. The [OpenAI blog post](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) describes the result — that a vortex tightens and spins ever faster while the fluid's energy stays bounded, a phenomenon known as finite-time blowup — and says the Lean formalisation "all but guarantees its correctness."

### Buckmaster cries foul

Within hours, NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster [published a statement](https://x.com/adiix_official/status/2097356260752433344) alleging OpenAI fought dirty. The timeline according to Buckmaster ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/openai-fought-dirty-on-career-making-math-problem-says-nyu-mathematician/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/08/openai-says-it-cracked-navier-stokes-math-grand-challenge-buckmaster-accusation-cheating-intimidation-tao-lament/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-solves-longstanding-math-problem-with-10-000-agent-swarm-but-cant-rule-out-benefitting-from-a-researchers-private-codex-data)): he and Levent Alpöge, an Anthropic researcher, had spent roughly a year using the Córdoba/Martínez-Zoroa approach to prove finite-time blowup for related fluid equations, achieving blowup for the 3D Euler equations by August 15 and completing Lean verification by August 22. Every draft lived inside private Codex sessions. On September 3 Buckmaster contacted OpenAI to clarify that the collaboration was personal, not an Anthropic project. On September 6, Sébastien Bubeck called to tell Buckmaster that OpenAI had independently solved the full Navier-Stokes problem using the same approach and proposed a joint announcement — but pushed to remove Alpöge from authorship because he works at Anthropic. Buckmaster refused. His central concern: the private Codex session data may have been accessible to OpenAI researchers who then announced a competing result using the same line of attack.

### Bubeck pushes back, Tao laments

Bubeck [publicly called](https://x.com/AGTPinsights/status/2097268066262397167) Buckmaster's allegations "false and inflammatory," insisting OpenAI's internal model solved the Euler equations independently by different means and that the Navier-Stokes extension, while following a similar method, was original work ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-claims-blockbuster-math-breakthrough-amid-swirl-of-controversy/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/08/openai-math-solution-navier-stokes-credit)). The core dispute remains: OpenAI cannot definitively rule out that it benefited from Buckmaster and Alpöge's private Codex data. Meanwhile, Terence Tao [wrote on Mastodon](https://fortune.com/2026/09/08/openai-says-it-cracked-navier-stokes-math-grand-challenge-buckmaster-accusation-cheating-intimidation-tao-lament/) that Buckmaster and Alpöge's work is a "remarkable achievement" and lamented AI companies turning longstanding mathematical challenges into marketing proof points. Jay Cummings captured the mood of the math community: ["I...worked on this proof for a year...and...they just…tweeted it out."](https://x.com/LongFormMath/status/2097404073406521691)

### Simon Willison's take

Simon Willison [blogged](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/8/on-navier-stokes/) about the announcement, noting the agents sent 2.7 million messages and used approximately 130 billion output tokens. His earlier [Hacker News comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49605691) on jyn's security essay is relevant context: if inference-time compute at this scale can crack millennium problems, the security implications of that same compute pointed at vulnerabilities are existential. [Quanta Magazine](https://www.quantamagazine.org/ai-has-solved-one-of-maths-1-million-millennium-prize-problems-20260908/) and [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02842-5) both covered the claim, with Nature noting that outside mathematicians still need to independently validate the result and that the Clay Mathematics Institute has not yet weighed in.

## Agentic Coding & Model Takes

### Mitsuhiko drops Astra for 5.6

Armin Ronacher [announced](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2097318251403395471) he is going back to GPT-5.6 for software engineering, calling Astra "absolutely amazing" but "the first time I feel like this is a genuine regression on OpenAI model release for my day to day workflows. Might even actually write down my thoughts about this." This follows his earlier [thread](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2096720787998650453) about Astra writing "weird Python slop" when code is one step removed from normal and producing "absolutely horrific" unit tests. Combined with Theo's "spikiest model ever" verdict from yesterday, the emerging consensus is that Astra is brilliant for reasoning, 3D generation and novel tasks but inconsistent for bread-and-butter software engineering — exactly the opposite of what most developers need day to day. Ronacher also published ["Latent Powers"](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/9/5/latent-powers/) on September 5, a blog post about using AI models (Kimi K3 and Sol) to flash custom firmware onto a Chinese CarPlay dongle, where the model suggested the project rather than Ronacher choosing it himself.

### OpenHands hits 1.0

[OpenHands](https://dev.to/jamilxt/openhands-just-hit-10-heres-how-to-run-it-on-your-own-machine-without-handing-over-the-keys-5666), the open-source autonomous coding agent, reached its 1.0 release with production-ready Docker sandboxing, built-in security policies, resource limits, and a plugin system. It scores 68% on SWE-bench Verified, and even with the smaller Devstral 24B model achieves 46.8% — matching what Devin 2.0 publicly reported. Self-hosted cost runs $0.20 to $1.05 per resolved task at H100 GPU rates depending on the backing model, making it significantly cheaper than commercial alternatives for organisations willing to run their own infrastructure.

### Matt Pocock and the skills ecosystem

Matt Pocock's [mattpocock/skills](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills) repo continues its climb — now at 238,000+ stars and over 18 million cumulative installs. Around September 3 he previewed a new `/show-me` skill that pushes agents toward diagrams and annotated diffs instead of long prose. His [argument from yesterday](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2096906181121818702) that knowledge work is much harder to automate than code, with its well-established feedback loops of types and tests, continues to draw discussion. His [follow-up](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2096980473754866081) quoting Kent C. Dodds: "Tactical programming is dead. Strategic programming has never been more vital."

## Business & Industry

### Nvidia confirms $12.9B Hugging Face acquisition

Nvidia [confirmed](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/nvidia-confirms-it-will-buy-hugging-face-for-12-9-billion/) a definitive agreement to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/nvidia-agrees-to-13-billion-deal-for-ai-platform-hugging-face), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/03/tech/nvidia-hugging-face-ai-acquisition), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/nvidia-agrees-to-buy-hugging-face-for-almost-13-billion-ai-expansion.html)). The deal includes $11.9 billion to shareholders plus $1 billion in equity to retain employees, and is expected to close in the first half of 2027. Hugging Face, which Nvidia had tried to acquire for $500 million the previous year, hosts over 3 million models, 500,000 datasets and 1 million applications used by 18 million developers. Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue approached Jensen Huang weeks before the deal.

### Anthropic IPO watch

Anthropic's IPO preparations continue at a [reported $965 billion post-money valuation](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/anthropic-ipo-2026-plans-september-or-early-otcober-listing-amid-965-billion-valuation-talks) following a $65 billion Series H in May, with bankers discussing potential listing valuations up to $2 trillion. The company confidentially filed its S-1 on June 1 and annualised revenue run rate exceeded $65 billion by end of July, up from roughly $9 billion at end of 2025. Google committed up to $40 billion at a $350 billion valuation with 5GW of Cloud capacity, and Amazon doubled up with $25 billion. An October listing is reportedly in play.

### Mistral's record European raise

[Mistral announced](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-makes-sovereign-open-weight-ai-to-frontier/) a €3 billion Series D at a post-money above €21 billion — the largest equity round ever by a European tech company. Samsung Electronics led, with participation from BlackRock, Advent, ASML, a16z, NVIDIA and others. The pitch is sovereignty: open weights and owned compute so customers are never locked to one vendor's roadmap.
