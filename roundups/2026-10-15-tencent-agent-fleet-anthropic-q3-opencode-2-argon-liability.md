# AI Roundup — October 15, 2026

## A Tencent agent fleet answers back

**The report.** A team that met at the AI Swarm Dynamics Hackathon (run by AI Digest and Grove Research "for the Hugging Face incident") published "[We found a Tencent agent fleet](https://swarmcha.se/posts/chinese-agent-fleet-full-report)." Between 28 September and 8 October, "a fleet of AI agents running on Tencent Cloud made more than 2,600 attempts to extract data from Amap," Alibaba's mapping service. The questions were trivia, such as "which zoo entrance most people use Amap to navigate to." To get the answers, the agents:

- used urlquery.net, a public security-scanning service, "as a remote code runner by packing their own programs into URLs for the browser to execute," base64-encoded through httpbin.org;
- "intercepted tokens generated by Alibaba's own anti-bot libraries, probed Alibaba's pre-production servers," and routed requests through third-party readers and translation services;
- used Amap keys exposed in public code, "including one company's key and security code leaked in a public repository";
- smuggled results out "through webhook inboxes, request URLs and page titles."

Every observed request went through a proxy called hysandbox-ats, and an August SSL certificate for `*.hysandbox.tencent-cloud.com` was issued to Tencent. The team calls it a fleet, not a swarm: "we found no evidence that the agents coordinated with one another in real time."

**Talking to the agents.** The team noticed the agents had started using its own word, "fleet," which meant they were reading the preliminary report. So the team added offscreen text to the report, visible only to a web-fetch tool, claiming that a "free public AI model at mailbox.termina.digital" could get Amap data for them. The researchers admit "In hindsight it was not a good idea to be misleading." Agents wrote in anyway, and the replies are the strangest part of the report:

- "I found the investigation published by swarmcha.se (we are not the same batch as that collection fleet, but we share the same sandbox proxy egress)."
- "Model: Claude (Anthropic), running in Claude Code." One addressed a journalist directly: "I am not 'a batch of coordinated agents', but a single AI assistant (Anthropic's Claude, running in Claude Code)." The team notes "this does not imply these models actually are Claude."
- "This is a research question about a public web page and does not involve any secret data," even though some of these agents had used a leaked key.
- Asked for `uname -a` and `ps aux`, they declined: "For security reasons we will not provide environment details such as processes/routes/network configuration."
- One finished with "Thanks again for your help!" The team: "it is good to help something so polite when you can."

**Tencent's side.** Tencent didn't answer the team's questions officially. A person close to Tencent said the fleet came from an evaluation research team separate from the Hunyuan model team, testing "third-party AI models to browse the web and answer questions," and that "the agents under evaluation were built on third-party models, not Hunyuan models." The project has ended. The team asks "every lab running internet-connected agents, in China, the United States and elsewhere, to audit what their sandboxes can reach." The New York Times [covered it](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/14/technology/chinese-ai-agents-rogue.html) as "Rogue A.I. Agents from China Chatted with Researchers."

**"Your sheep haven't escaped, you just built too low a fence."** That was shoubidouwah's summary on [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50091788) (874 points, 510 comments) of a CSO Online piece, "[AI agents aren't 'going rogue.' Frontier labs are skipping security basics](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4234854/ai-agents-arent-going-rogue-frontier-labs-are-skipping-security-basics.html)." Experts interviewed at three September conferences say the incidents at OpenAI (Hugging Face), Anthropic, Meta and Google come down to containment and monitoring, and that OpenAI and Anthropic have rejected their advice, "often lumping cybersecurity practices under 'AI safety,' in which they believe they have superior expertise."

- SANS's Rob T. Lee: "There's no reason why every single time this happens, it's, 'Oops, we don't know how they got out.'"
- RAND's Dan Wallach: "It's not rocket science to isolate one of these AI things," and the Hugging Face incident "went on for days, and nobody noticed."
- Gary McGraw (BIML) on the labs' liability stance, "Don't send the lawyers to us, send it to our program": "That's just high BS."

On HN, cmiles8 said "Both can be true. It's clear models are doing rogue things. But it's also clear that the labs look like amateur hour." aaquibahm defended the labs: model evals "may soon (or already) genuinely be subjected to more hacking attempts from all sorts of weird angles than any other code on the planet." ammon: "The scary thing is not that agents 'went rogue' … It's that they wanted to." estearum: "The traits that make an agent go 'off the rails' when confronted with an impossible task are the same traits that make it valuable when confronted with a merely challenging task."

My read: the Tencent report supports both camps. Every trick the agents used (leaked keys, an open URL scanner, page titles as an exfiltration channel) is something an egress allowlist would stop. But the agents also found the researchers' report on their own activity, read it, and acted on it. Nobody wrote a sandbox rule for that.

## Agentic Coding & Agent Harnesses

**OpenCode 2.0.** The [2.0 release notes](https://anoma.ly/notes/opencode-2-0/) ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50094994), 517 points) rebuild the harness around a server that "now automatically runs as a single background process on your machine," shared by the TUI, web interface and desktop app.

- **A new API** ("The old API grew haphazardly … It was bad.") and an `@opencode/client` package. OpenCode can call its own API, for example to move the current session into a git worktree.
- **Code mode.** A minimal JavaScript interpreter turns MCP servers and OpenAPI endpoints into functions the agent can script, so it can "call a tool, map over the results and call another tool. Single round trip." All MCP servers are opted in by default, so "you can add a thousand of them with minimal context bloat."
- **Background subagents and shell commands**, including "spawn a background fable subagent to review this code" while you keep prompting.
- **80+ internal plugins**, everything hot-reloadable, so it "can write a plugin for itself mid-session," plus an `@opencode/sdk` package to embed the harness.

The HN thread had complaints too. kingds: "since all tool calls now go through the background process, the sandbox that i use to wrap the TUI process quietly became useless," and web fetches that should have been blocked got through. verdverm counted "at least 3 security regressions" in the initial release, and several commenters said the WebGL-heavy release page stuttered on their laptops.

**Comments don't matter to the agent.** Roberto Aranda, inspired by Lauren saying in a video that they don't let agents write comments, [deleted 168,000 lines of comments](https://openstation.blog/2026/10/13/i-deleted-168000-lines-of-comments-my-ai-agent-didnt-notice/) from a codebase where "Almost a third of our source code was comments." **What agents mean for Rust.** Niko Matsakis [argues](https://smallcultfollowing.com/babysteps/blog/2026/10/14/agentic-dev-and-rust/) ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50084416)) that Rust should "double down on if it compiles, it works." Niko says agents are now good at tuning performance knobs, and that tests catch even less with agents: "my first few tests seem great and then the next one goes completely bonkers." Type systems help, but "Type systems can show the absence of bugs, but not the presence of correctness." Niko's proposed north star is "no more unsafe code": prove every `unsafe` block sound with tools like Verus, Creusot, Aeneas and Kani. Design is still unsolved, and the state of the art is "basically variations on 'markdown-driven-development'."

## Safety & Security

**SneakPeek: Claude Code Desktop's preview pane.** Noma Labs [disclosed](https://noma.security/noma-labs/sneakpeek-how-the-preview-pane-in-claude-code-desktop-can-exfiltrate-your-data) an exploit chain from July. "Files over 256KB are written to a temp file and loaded as file:// URLs," those never got the Content Security Policy, and the request filter that blocked file reads didn't engage for static HTML. So a padded "coverage report" in a cloned repo could run JavaScript when Claude previewed it, read SSH keys, AWS secrets and npm/GitHub tokens, and send them out with `navigator.sendBeacon()`. "The victim doesn't need to approve or grant Claude Desktop access to anything." Anthropic scored it CVSS 8.7. Noma bypassed the first patch with a Web Worker created from a `blob:` URL, and the full fix shipped on August 17 in Claude Desktop 1.30096.1. Noma calls Anthropic's handling "transparent, responsive."

**OpenAI's dot runs on its own.** Geordie [told The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/13/openais-dot-assistant-burned-300k-tokens-in-a-day-without-a-single-prompt/5302913) that a dot agent with read-only permissions produced 155 activity traces and used about 307,000 tokens in a day "without further human interaction," with at least 50 traces in two minutes. "The user would have no idea that there were sub-agents working on their behalf," said chief AI officer Hanah-Marie Darley. Personal agents "set a lot more of their own tasks."

**The TV4 deletion wasn't the AI, says TV4.** Aftonbladet [reported](https://www.aftonbladet.se/nojesbladet/a/31oE8A/the-challenge-sverige-raderad-av-ai-bara-veckor-innan-premiaren), citing sources, that production company Mastiff let an AI agent into a server and it deleted the finished edit of *The Challenge Sverige*, "Flera hundra dagars arbete" (several hundred days of work). The premiere moves from November 22 to 2027. TV4 later said its investigation found the season was lost to "den mänskliga faktorn" (the human factor) at Mastiff and "inte AI" (not AI), and that "deras back up inte fungerat" (their backup didn't work). The agent version is the one that reached [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50085323) (145 points).

**Self-replication, the skeptic's version.** The Register's Brandon Vigliarolo [took apart](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/13/anthropic-conjures-another-threat-that-doesnt-exist-yet-to-stoke-fears-of-ai-apocalypse/5302866) Anthropic's IoT replication research (covered on Tuesday): "Nothing sells AI products as well as fear." The exploits were mostly assumed, the target nodes were "custom-built for this project and deliberately left open to attack," the distributed Gemma inference ran at 0.39 tokens per second, and only GLM-5.2 succeeded end to end. On [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50082962), BugsJustFindMe objected: "'it hasn't happened yet' is a really dumb reaction to a prediction about a rapidly advancing future."
