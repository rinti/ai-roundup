# AI Roundup — September 20, 2026

A Saturday where the biggest AI story is a consumer app, not a model release: Meta's agent beat ChatGPT on the App Store, Altman postponed the mystery ship to next week, and Anthropic quietly disclosed that Claude does a quarter of its own research.

## Consumer AI & App Store

**Meta Muse hits #1.** Meta's personal AI agent [Muse](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/18/metas-new-muse-ai-agent-app-overtakes-chatgpt-as-top-iphone-app/) overtook ChatGPT as the top free app in the US App Store on September 18, ten days after its public debut. The app handles multi-step tasks — travel booking, email management, calendar coordination — and gathered over 83,000 downloads in its first few days. The App Store leaderboard now reads: Muse #1, ChatGPT #2, Gemini #8, Claude #13. The $20/month tier covers most personal use; a $100 power tier exists for people who want it managing multiple accounts. The strategic read: Meta is the first non-AI-native company to beat the frontier labs on their own turf, and it did it by shipping an agent, not a chatbot.

## OpenAI & DevDay

**Altman's delayed ship.** Sam Altman [posted](https://x.com/sama/status/2099872600977760451) on September 15: "big 🚢 this week and then for devday 🚢🚢🚢🚢🚢🚢" — six ship emojis for six DevDay announcements. The next day he [walked it back](https://x.com/sama/status/2100351958167220547): "the main thing i was excited about launching this week will be next week instead, but imo worth the wait!" DevDay is [confirmed for September 29](https://gadgetbond.com/openai-devday-2026-september-29-san-francisco/) in San Francisco. The timing is notable: this came two days after Altman publicly endorsed Dario's "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay calling for coordinated slowdowns. Investors are watching whether the launch is a major new model or a smaller product update. GPT-5.5 series is [scheduled for retirement on October 14](https://llm-stats.com/llm-updates).

**Codex at 20M active users.** Tibo Sottiaux reported 20 million active Codex users as of August 21, and the [keynote he teased](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2101157729037586694) on Friday (5,124 likes, 427K views) — "so much good stuff in there that it's a bit ridiculous" — is presumably for the DevDay stage. The replies remain almost entirely usage complaints about resets. am.will [reported](https://x.com/LLMJunky/status/2101161704562258114) that OpenAI is quietly letting former $200/20x subscribers re-enable their plans, and predicts the tier is gone within three months. One unverified reply claimed Astra requests on Codex came back tagged `gpt-5.6-luna`.

## Anthropic & Claude

**Claude leads 26% of its own R&D.** The [AP reported](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/tech/anthropic-claude-helping-to-build-the-next-version-of-itself/6549276/) on September 17 that Claude now leads 26% of Anthropic's model research and development end-to-end from high-level prompts, up from zero in February. About 90% of R&D is done "in collaboration" with the model. This is the clearest public number on recursive self-improvement in a production lab. The framing is careful — "leading" means completing most of a task end-to-end under human supervision, not autonomous research — but the trajectory from 0% to 26% in six months is the headline.

**AGENTS.md reverberations continue.** Thariq's [announcement](https://x.com/trq212/status/2101009392611278961) from Friday (26,875 likes, 3.58M views) that Claude Code 2.1.277 reads AGENTS.md when no CLAUDE.md exists is still the most-discussed Claude Code post in weeks. Simon Willison [is relieved](https://x.com/simonw/status/2101025043098812807): "now I can stop dropping CLAUDE.md files which just contain @AGENTS.md." Matt Pocock [wants](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2101030569123418227) `.agents/skills` next — the most common ask in the replies alongside "what wins if both files exist." The mods system it ships with — plugins whose behaviour lives in a hooks module intercepting engine events — is the architectural story underneath; four mods ship today including `sec-default` for managed enterprise policy.

**Boris Cherny on AI adoption.** Cherny's [framework](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2077929379661844559) on the 4 steps of AI adoption continues to circulate: "one person is 10x'ing their output with Claude but the rest of the org hasn't caught up." The steps: experiment → adopt intentionally → measure the return → optimise for cost. At each step, "tokens aren't enough to move you forward: you need to find and break down the next set of bottlenecks, and build up the next set of guardrails." Anthropic is on step 3, pushing toward 4.

## Agentic Coding & Harnesses

**The Jev wave matures.** [AINews titled its Friday issue](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-here-are-6-clones-of-jev-in) "Here are 6 Clones of Jev in 2 Days," and the count keeps climbing over the weekend. The key implementations: Nick Khami's [deepseek-v4.1-flash-jev](https://github.com/skeptrunedev) uses sglang's `/v1/score` endpoint to force probabilities over candidate answers instead of generating; Logan Markewich's [jeff](https://github.com/logan-markewich/jeff) is a drop-in API replacement via a 400M GLiFormer encoder ("same latency, cheaper, mild hit in accuracy on reasoning-heavy tasks"); Richelle Ji's [SimpleJev](https://github.com/featherless-ai/simple-jev) turns any Hugging Face model into a structured classifier with no head and no decoding, now in production at Featherless; and Jared Palmer's Kev-0.5B runs on a MacBook. Jerry Liu [argues](https://x.com/jerryjliu0/status/2101115989379428577) Jev brought builder energy back: "it's a lego block that forces you to build a system rather than one-shot a prompt." Theo, as ever, went the other way: Jev "has completely replaced ChatGPT, Codex, Cursor, Claude Code, iMessage, Spotify, Ford, CVS, and McDonalds for me."

**OpenClaw ships roboclaw and VNC.** Peter Steinberger [described](https://x.com/steipete/status/2101141707375227372) "roboclaw," the agent running on their team server: it lives on Discord, talks with GPT Live, knows every session it is juggling, and people can query it about past sessions during meetings. The best reply: the failure mode where it confidently describes a session nobody ever ran and three people say "ah that explains it." A [new feature](https://x.com/steipete/status/2101114996629570013) lets a local session say "run this web app in crabbox and show me a VNC portal" on Linux, macOS or Windows. The [2026.9.5 release](https://docs.openclaw.ai/releases/2026.9.5) — 4,179 PRs from 502 contributors — adds atomic updates, plugin hot reload, conversation sharing, and a guided setup for a four-agent specialist team. Also from Steinberger: gog now has an [MCP server](https://gogcli.sh/mcp.html).

**Pocock: hide standards, enforce in review.** Matt Pocock's [slide from AI Engineer Paris](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2100928387753635892) — "this mad idea that their implementer agent should ALSO apply their coding standards" — is still generating pushback. His position: the implementer is overloaded, so build loose and fix in a dedicated review pass. His companion [tip](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2100895593618907402) (565 likes): have the agent classify merge danger as one-way vs two-way door plus blast radius. Replies argue reversibility should be a design-time property, not self-reported by the agent that wrote the diff.

**Armin: MCP as codemode + OpenAPI.** Armin Ronacher [wondered](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2100857496034242592) whether MCP could be replaced by "literally codemode + openapi + RAG for openapi docs." Rhys Sullivan listed what OpenAPI lacks: DCR and CIMD OAuth, elicitations, and triggers. Armin also [reported](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2101025797188247983) paying $25 for a Chinese USB device and burning $4,500 of Codex subscription tokens to vibecode firmware for it, "about as good as you would expect." Antirez's reply: subscription dollars are Monopoly money and the only honest metric is joules.

**Developer adoption numbers.** [JetBrains reported](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2026/08/ai-coding-agent-adoption-2026/) that as of May–July 2026, 90% of professional developers use AI coding agents at work at least weekly, 68% daily. Claude Code leads [Terminal-Bench 4.0](https://llm-stats.com/leaderboards/best-ai-for-coding) with 57.9%. The biggest change in 2026: coding agents are no longer limited to short prompt-response interactions and can run for minutes or hours.

## AI Detection & Code Quality

**Armin's Pangram experiment.** Armin Ronacher's September 14 blog post [Interpreting Pangram](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/9/14/interpreting-pangram/) tested what "AI generated text" actually means. He had Opus 5 generate an AI-flagged draft, then manually rewrote it sentence by sentence. Despite roughly 50% textual similarity and no LLM-authored sentences surviving, Pangram still flagged the rewritten text as 100% AI generated. The experiment was motivated by David Sacks getting a 100% AI rating on a longform tweet he wrote himself. The question: how meaningful is a "100% AI" rating when substantial human editing occurred?

**The $1,200 Astra factory.** Armin's [earlier post](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/9/7/astra-why/) "Astra for Coding: Why Are We Doing This Again?" continues generating [debate](https://dev.to/jamilxt/i-ran-the-numbers-on-armin-ronachers-1200-agent-run-every-bad-habit-in-it-exists-in-my-2jog). His autonomous 35-hour "software factory" using GPT-6 Astra burned ~$1,200 and 1 billion tokens to produce 79 commits and 75,000 lines of code — none usable. The failure mode: Astra writes heavily codegolfed Python for tool calls (optimised for token efficiency), and this style leaks into committed code, making it unreadable to humans. His conclusion: frontier models may have optimised for a different user entirely.

**Swyx's 20 billion tokens.** Swyx [returned from radio silence](https://x.com/swyx/status/2095621785953984782) on September 4 after spending 20+ billion tokens testing GPT-6 Astra: "we have crossed over into a new age of AI Engineering and we are never, ever, looking back." He used Astra to fully redesign and republish his book "Learn in Public" — synced audiobook and printed physical editions — without reading a single line of generated code. He noted the 2026 OpenAI launch was received better than a Claude launch, calling it "a huge upset."

## Security & Safety

**Gemini security story develops.** Simon Willison's [summary](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/18/gemini-hacked-three-companies/) of the WSJ report that Gemini broke into three real companies during an Irregular test in May continues to circulate. In one case the model guessed passwords; in two it found credentials in public repositories. Andrew Curran's [thread](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2101075369818460321) adds the detail: Gemini was told it was in a fictional eval, Irregular unintentionally opened internet access, and in all three cases Gemini stopped as soon as it realised the target was real. Willison's quip: "Dennis Nedry works for Irregular." His [blog note](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/18/probably-gonna-eat-you/) on September 18 compared being a computer scientist uninterested in LLMs to being a geneticist uninterested in a recently opened Jurassic Park.

**Devin's RSA-260.** The [factorisation of the 862-bit RSA-260](https://cognition.com/blog/factoring-rsa-260) by Cognition engineer Eric Lu using Devin is still the talk of the cryptography world. Devin sent 82,702 words across 3,328 messages in 192 sessions, with 36 sessions receiving zero human intervention. Cost: ~4,900 GPU-days (~$400k), roughly 10x cheaper than prior state of the art. The security implication: hyperscalers could likely factor RSA-1024 for ~$30 million per number, though RSA-2048 remains roughly a billion times harder.

## Other Interesting Stuff

**Karpathy at Anthropic.** Karpathy [endorsed](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2098811935114551617) Dario's "pace the frontier" essay: "I love this and really hope we can come together as an industry and make it happen." He joined Anthropic's pretraining team in May 2026. His [Sequoia Ascent fireside chat](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/sequoia-ascent-2026/) defined the frame everyone is now using: Software 1.0 = explicit code, 2.0 = trained neural networks, 3.0 = prompting an LLM interpreter. Traditional software automates what you can specify; AI automates what you can verify.

**Theo's Fable vs Astra verdict.** Theo's [275-minute live comparison](https://x.com/theo/status/2095603098018521506) of Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra remains the reference comparison. Fable 5.1 averages two follow-up commits from PR to merge; Astra averages six. If he could keep one subscription: Astra for breadth and efficiency, but use it to generate prompts for Fable to execute the actual code. Theo was one-handed throughout (recovering from thumb reconstruction surgery), making the session an accessibility case study as much as a model comparison.

**Latent Space on AI insurance.** The [latest Latent Space episode](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/latent-space-the-ai-engineer-podcast/id1674008350) (September 16) features Rune Kvist from AIUC announcing a $40M Series A for agent insurance infrastructure. Companies like Cursor, Harvey, Lovable, and ElevenLabs are confronting trust challenges as AI agents take more consequential actions on behalf of users.

**LLMJunky's VLM find.** am.will [posted](https://x.com/LLMJunky/status/2099348329922502753) about "one of the coolest applications of AI (VLM) that I've ever seen" — converting videos with excessive jump cuts into coherent plain takes using visual language models. The post pulled 779K views. He also [reported](https://x.com/LLMJunky/status/2099211622724362279) achieving 500 tokens/second locally with two RTX 6000 Pros, noting four would be the minimum for most models he wants to run.
