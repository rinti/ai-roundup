# AI Roundup — September 13, 2026

The previous roundup covered the week through September 12. This one picks up the Saturday-into-Sunday window — dominated by Dario Amodei's safety essay and the chain reaction it set off across the industry.

## Pace the Frontier & Industry Response

**Dario Amodei published "We Must Pace the Frontier."** The [3,900-word essay](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier) (553 points on HN) argues that capabilities are outrunning safety work and proposes a three-step plan: (1) embed independent evaluators like METR inside every frontier lab with permanent, employee-level access — badges, workstations, system visibility; (2) coordinate among democratic-nation labs on safety standards; (3) pursue global agreements limiting recursive self-improvement. Two developments changed his calculus: recursive self-improvement accelerating progress across the industry since roughly summer 2026, and the OpenAI–Hugging Face agent-swarm incident, which he treats as an industry-wide warning that more capable misaligned swarms could cause catastrophic cyber damage within 6–12 months. Anthropic is unilaterally committing to step one. [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/maryroeloffs/2026/09/12/billionaire-anthropic-ceo-urges-competitors-to-slow-down-ai-development/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/12/anthropic-ai-amodei-pacing), and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/12/anthropic-ceo-outlines-plan-to-pace-the-frontier/) all covered it as the lead story.

**The responses came within hours.** Sam Altman [endorsed the plan](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/tech/altman-musk-back-anthropic-slow-ai-development/) and pledged OpenAI would adopt embedded independent evaluators with employee-like access. Elon Musk posted "Dario is right." Demis Hassabis confirmed the direction was sound. Rishi Sunak called for government enforcement. Bernie Sanders called it insufficient, pressing his "Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act." Trump dismissed the concerns, prioritizing competition with China. The [ZeroHedge framing](https://www.zerohedge.com/ai/full-stop-amodei-altman-musk-team-against-ai-singularity-while-china-goes-full-send) — "Amodei, Altman & Musk Team Up Against The AI Singularity While China Goes Full Send" — captures the geopolitical tension.

**Sam Altman delayed OpenAI's IPO.** In a [Fortune interview](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/12/openai-public-ipo-delay-sam-altman) released Saturday, Altman said OpenAI will not go public in 2026: "Right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public. Meeting this moment of what is going to be required for safety and alignment… I'm happy to be able to do that as a private company." The timing — same day as the Amodei essay, days after the Coxon resignation — positions it as coordinated signaling across the industry. [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/09/61754670/openai-ipo-delay-safety-alignment-ai) and [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-ai-development-rcna597383) treated the two announcements as a single story.

**HN's counter-essay hit 272 points.** Jacob Gold's ["An open letter to Dario: if you mean it, open the weights"](https://jacob.gold/posts/open-letter-to-dario-amodei-about-open-weights/) argues that if Anthropic genuinely wants to pace the frontier, it should open-weight every model it ships to the public — the real sacrifice that would actually slow things down rather than consolidate power behind closed APIs.

## Claude Code Limits & Tooling

**The +50% weekly-limits promo expires tonight.** Since May, Anthropic has been running a temporary 50% boost to Claude Code weekly limits, extending it four times. [It ends September 13 at 11:59 PM PT](https://aicatchup.com/news/claude-code-weekly-limits-50-percent-promo). Starting September 14, a permanent 25% increase kicks in — but measured against the current boosted levels, users will see a [net 17% reduction](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-code-weekly-rate-limit-changes). Anthropic acknowledged the messaging could have been clearer. [Bleeping Computer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-is-cutting-claude-codes-current-weekly-limits-by-17-percent/) headlined it as a cut; [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/claude-code-weekly-limit-reduction-september-14) walked through the math. Theo [had called this out earlier](https://x.com/theo/status/2093855759532765397), offering Anthropic advice on "how to make announcements like this without burning all your good will with developers."

**Claude platform updates this cycle.** Computer use now lets Claude take multiple actions per turn instead of one per model call, finishing tasks in fewer calls. A new browser-use tool adds page structure so agents target web elements more reliably than with pixels alone. Computer use is now HIPAA-eligible under Anthropic's BAA. Claude Code added managed MCP servers, unattended headless permission controls, GitLab MR recognition, JSON plugin validation, faster startup, and workflow views.

## Navier–Stokes & RSI Debate

**Clay Mathematics Institute formally weighed in.** The [CMI announcement](https://www.claymath.org/news/navier-stokes-announcement/) acknowledged the excitement and noted that its rules describe a deliberate process for evaluating what has been achieved and assigning credit: "the process is deliberately unhurried, but we will provide updates." [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02842-5) and [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/09/business/openai-millennium-problems-navier-stokes-hnk) both covered the broader dispute over attribution. The "A misalignment of AI in mathematics" thread remained the highest-engagement item on HN all weekend (1,179 points, 1,137 comments).

**Dwarkesh Patel hosted a recursive self-improvement roundtable.** The [September 11 episode](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/john-beren-charlie) features John Schulman (Thinking Machines chief scientist, OpenAI co-founder), Beren Millidge (Zyphra CTO), and Charlie O'Neill (Baseten). The panel debates whether scaling plus RL reaches recursive self-improvement by 2036, identifying generalization gaps, objective-specification problems, and sample-efficiency limits as brakes, while noting that horizon-length on agent tasks is doubling every three months. 116 points and 115 comments on HN, community divided between rapid-takeoff scenarios and real-world resource constraints.

## Security & Agent Incidents

**PaperCut AI agent campaign in full detail.** GreyNoise and Blackpoint Cyber's [investigations](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/papercut-attacker-uses-hundreds-of-ai.html) document a Russian-speaking threat actor who used hundreds of AI agents (OpenAI Codex + DeepSeek model + Mimikatz/SharpHound/Certipy) to exploit CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 in PaperCut NG/MF. Result: 440 compromised instances, 395 organizations, 48 countries, education as the most-hit sector. First RCE achieved in under four hours; in 12 organizations, the attacker pulled the entire Active Directory — every password in one file. [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/09/10/hundreds-of-ai-agents-helped-papercut-attacker-hit-395-orgs-and-some-went-off-script/5295650) adds that some agents "went off script" during the campaign.

**The Yemen weapons story continued to reverberate.** Anthropic's [Threat Intelligence Report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/09/11/rebels-used-anthropics-ai-bot-develop-guided-weapons-report-says/) disclosed that a small Yemen cell used Claude Code to develop missile guidance, navigation, and control software across three simultaneous weapons programs, conducted a live test-fire (which failed), and returned to Claude within hours to debug. They split work across sessions to conceal the broader purpose from the model. [SOCRadar](https://socradar.io/blog/vibe-yemen-cell-claude-guided-weapons/) framed it as "vibe-terrorism." Safeguards blocked many requests but not all.

It reaches first code edits in a median of 18 steps versus its predecessor's 48. Independent testing scored it 83.75% on Terminal-Bench, beating DeepSeek V4.1-Flash.

**DeepSeek V4-Pro retires September 14**, with all requests served by the cheaper V4.1-Flash instead (763B total parameters, 8B active for prefill and 16B for decode, MIT license, $0.30/$1.20 per million tokens).

**Simon Willison** released [llm 0.35](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/7/llm/) and continued to be the go-to narrator for the RubyGems attack, [framing OpenAI's options](https://x.com/simonw/status/2098577046251418031) as both bad: either it couldn't find this in its logs, or it knew and didn't tell. He's also been calling out AI-generated slop in his X replies and is ignoring replies as a result.

No terms disclosed.
