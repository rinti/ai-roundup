### Thariq's AI-native dock, and forks that keep merging upstream

Thariq's [personal project](https://x.com/trq212/status/2115135857669881939) got the biggest reaction of the day among the tracked accounts (8.5K likes, 596K views): "an AI-native macOS dock with custom modes (writing, building, etc.) to help me stay focused". The agent "is able to customize mode behavior and add new modes, it uses jev to classify my activity + notifications in real time & act on them." Thariq [isn't sharing it yet](https://x.com/trq212/status/2115135859075015137): "there are still many edge cases (would not recommend using)", but "this is the kind of HCI work I've been wanting to do for a while, but messing with OS internals has always been very time consuming". Thariq then [asked](https://x.com/trq212/status/2115188992861970693) for "a new way to distribute opensource software such that people can modify via agents but also get upstream automatically merged in", because "coding agents can do this but they won't do it automatically" (2.6K likes). Replies pointed to existing attempts. Kieran Klaassen linked [ghuntley.com/remix](https://ghuntley.com/remix/), Fabian [mentioned](https://x.com/fabrohl/status/2115199332161036722) Theo's PATCH.md, and dex [cited](https://x.com/dexhorthy/status/2115215717662609500) the Kubernetes kustomize design and a "design-iterated-loop" skill for keeping a fork current "while preserving our changes". ### Matt Pocock wants a word for tasks agents can't get wrong

"[I feel like I need a word for the type of task that agents are really good at](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2115035446627459461)" (1.2K likes, 456 replies): tasks "where it has 100% of the data, it can run experiments, verify independently, and hill-climb towards its destination … On these tasks my trust level is totally maxed-out". Mario Zechner's "[agent-complete](https://x.com/badlogicgames/status/2115039679712219175)" was the top reply (187 likes). Philo Groves [suggested](https://x.com/PhiloGroves/status/2115045816591110392) "Verifiable", noting that "Many tasks may seem unverifiable right now, until we build the verifiers." Matt [leaned that way](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2115095830797406478) with a caveat: "some tasks are partially verifiable, which is where agents get into trouble thinking they're verified them when it's actually half-finished. I'm thinking like '100% verifiable'." Other candidates: "Legible", "Known", "claudable", "latchkey" tasks, and "[they shall be pocock tasks](https://x.com/lumirth/status/2115143157885374871)".

Earlier, Matt posted [an example](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2115023694942662985) of domain knowledge paying off (1.4K likes). While building a video editor, Matt knew "certain consonants can fiddle around with silence detection" and told the agent. "The word 'fricative' is enough for it to start busting out full IPA … Turns out, knowing things is really fucking valuable." The feature is [an LLM pass](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2115030431208816848) "over the deterministic detection to edit for rhythm and cadence." Andy Wojcicki [took it](https://x.com/pretendsmarts/status/2115052957607592084) as an argument against the "cavemen" skills: "Precise language is there for a reason".

## Claude & Anthropic

### Felix Rieseberg retires the coding interview

"[The coding interview is dead, at least for my team at Anthropic. I've seen enough to know that it no longer provides signal. I removed it from loops this month.](https://x.com/felixrieseberg/status/2115200995546566910)" (8.3K likes, 876K views). The accompanying post, [Hunger beats tenure](https://felixrieseberg.com/hunger-beats-tenure-advice-for-junior-engineers/), says "There's no more point in giving you a little React challenge than there is in quizzing people about assembly; Claude is now better at both", and that newer engineers now "fly past people who are probably better coders and have been around much longer." The advice for engineers "without social capital, status, or power": pick business impact over backlog, pick a radical idea, build it over a weekend, focus on one good idea, and lean into a fresh perspective. Felix's example: two relatively new engineers, Anthony Morris and Alice Zhao, built a working prototype of a graphical Claude Code over one weekend, and "The first public version shipped roughly eight days later." Felix hasn't found a replacement yet and is "scanning resumes and portfolios for little prototypes that exhibit taste, empathy, and principled thinking."

Lauren (@poteto) [replied](https://x.com/poteto/status/2115217460836422061) "love it! thanks for leading the way" and Thariq "[++](https://x.com/trq212/status/2115205249405464649)". The doubters: "[Most companies and organizations aren't anthropic and will be doing them 7 years from now lmao](https://x.com/NeuralAA/status/2115216251484086653)". Jeremy Bernier [said](https://x.com/jeremybernier/status/2115217385481269475) they did "a take-home coding test for Anthropic last week" and almost wished for the traditional interview, "b/c then at least I'd know what to expect".

### Claude in drug development: six partners and its own programs

Anthropic [published](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2115178675050307942) [early results](https://www.anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development) from research partnerships with BigHat Biosciences, Bristol Myers Squibb, Divergence Labs, Enveda, Generate Biomedicines and The Column Group (7.9K likes, 1.7M views). It has also started its own drug discovery work, "including programs for rare childhood forms of muscular dystrophy, where Claude handles much of the scientific and operational work." The case studies:

- **Generate Biomedicines:** six scientists used Claude Code to redesign part of a CAR-T therapy. One design was "about 70% shorter than the standard version" and let immune cells make "roughly 10 times as many receptors".
- **Divergence Labs** (food allergies): Claude "largely autonomously, generated roughly 35,000 antibody designs in about a month".
- **BigHat:** Claude, connected to the lab's data system, proposed changes that the lab turned into about 1,010 variants in about 10 weeks. Partway through, Claude and the Anthropic researcher decided the standard heat-stability and clumping metrics were misleading and proposed different ones.
- **Enveda:** over 1,061 pages of manufacturing documentation, Claude "caught all 11 issues that human reviewers had spotted—and flagged seven they'd missed", and cut review time by 89%. A regulatory document draft took four hours instead of "about three months by hand".
- **The Column Group** is starting Pleiades, a company that will use Claude to run more drug programs than usual for a small team. BMS is using Claude to estimate how likely an early-stage trial is to succeed.

On [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50265609) (211 points), people questioned the line "we need to work with many more biotechnology firms and integrate Claude more deeply into their development processes" ("the wording here feels very self-serving"). enraged_camel's explanation for why biology moves slower than math: "the verification loops are much longer in applied sciences."

### Physicists take custody of OpenAI's Yang-Mills proof

OpenAI's claimed proof of the existence of quantum chromodynamics (covered yesterday) now has a custodian. KITP [posted](https://x.com/KITP_UCSB/status/2115236165330846045) a [statement](https://www.kitp.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/YM_working_group.pdf) from the committee that will handle it: Michael R. Douglas, David J. Gross (chair), Arthur Jaffe, Igor R. Klebanov and Nikita Nekrasov. On October 16, OpenAI asked Gross "to assume custody of the proof, decide on the best way to release it to the community, and shepherd the release process", and the committee says it has "no responsibility to OpenAI." The statement calls the proof of quantum Yang-Mills "including coupling to fermions" "an important step towards putting QFT on a rigorous mathematical footing" and credits Tadeusz Balaban's renormalization-group work from the 1980s. But "confinement and the mass gap remain very important open challenges", which, as @aiSignalDrift put it, means "not the full Millennium problem yet". The committee will first check that the Lean formalization's definitions and problem statement match standard physics and mathematics, release the Lean proof after that, and publish "the full 1147-page LLM proofs" with its assessment.

Several replies objected to the withholding. Francesco Maggi [wrote](https://x.com/Frances01896069/status/2115307977414439154) that "Once a public claim of a major scientific discovery is made, the evidence supporting that claim should be made public", and @aiSignalDrift [asked](https://x.com/aiSignalDrift/status/2115238198368489609) "Why not publish the Lean theorem statement now so anyone can audit what was actually claimed?"

## Models & Benchmarks

### Artificial Analysis v5: private tests, and the open-weights gap grows to 12 points

Artificial Analysis [released](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2115265133467635877) Intelligence Index v5 (3.2K likes, 569K views), with "60% of the Index … now based on evaluations with private test sets or held-out answers." New additions are Cognition's private FrontierCode 2.0 ("graded on whether a repository maintainer would merge the agent's change") and Terminal-Bench-Science. HLE-Diamond replaces Humanity's Last Exam, and GDPval-AA, SciCode and AA-LCR are gone. Claude Opus 5.5 (max) leads at 58, ahead of GPT-6 Astra (57) and GPT-6.1 Sol (55), with Gemini 4 Argon next at 50. The open-weights gap went from 3 points to 12. Kimi K3.1 Pro (weights not yet released) scores 46, and the best released weights, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 and GLM-5.3, score 32, below Claude Haiku 5.5 at 36. GPT-6.1 Terra was the cheapest top-10 model at $0.96 per task, and Fable 5.1 the most expensive at $13.04.

Many replies didn't believe the ranking. The top one: "[sonnet 5.5 and 6.1 terra being above fable 5.1 here doesn't exactly give me confidence](https://x.com/synthwavedd/status/2115265887230148694)". - dax: "[there's few people betting on opensource models more than us … but too many people are acting like they're on the verge of killing frontier](https://x.com/thdxr/status/2115304892919099501)". @mSanterre [replied](https://x.com/mSanterre/status/2115307398894367160) that "Kimi k3.1 pro is just as good as fable for code".
