# AI Roundup — November 25, 2026

## T3 Code's Telemetry Fight

- **The audit.** A long Chinese-language post by [陈大黄](https://x.com/realchendahuang/status/2125153472450527440) (15,568 likes, 2.0M views) walked through T3 Code's git history and started all of this. Its claims: a March PR added default-on PostHog telemetry with no docs, popup or toggle, while the homepage kept saying "No telemetry" and "Unless you opt in. Full stop." for another three months. An October PR routed events through T3's own `ingest.t3.codes` endpoint, which keeps a copy before forwarding to PostHog. A second October PR read `~/.codex/auth.json` and `~/.claude.json`, took the OpenAI user ID and Claude account UUID, and sent unsalted SHA-256 hashes of them. The post also says maintainers closed four community PRs that added a settings toggle, and quotes maintainer Julius closing an issue with "We will not make this easier to opt out of, it is crucial for us to know how many users we have". Opting out takes two environment variables. The top reply, from [@4isSecure](https://x.com/4isSecure/status/2125166570276102183): "if the product is free, you are the product."
- **It escalates.** [@sabotaj0707](https://x.com/sabotaj0707/status/2125210190005846326) (5,724 likes): "Checked the repo myself. It's real." And then: "I live in Germany, so I just filed a complaint with our DPA (LfD Sachsen-Anhalt) and tipped @NOYBeu & @heiseonline." The most-liked reply came from [@ItsSnazzie](https://x.com/ItsSnazzie/status/2125249225349075272): "Theo will activate his special move, the block button." [Anthony Ronning](https://x.com/anthonyronning/status/2125274188751548650) (3,164 likes) went much further: "T3 Code is a honey pot. Their default remote control options give @theo RCE level permissions into every computer with it turned on." That one drew a community note saying T3 Connect is optional, traffic goes through a Cloudflare Tunnel, and the relay "brokers sign-in only, not plaintext session messages". [Theo's reply](https://x.com/theo/status/2125319671561994578): "Everything you reported here is also the case for Claude Code lmao. It's very basic tunnel usage."
- **Theo's first hour.** It did not start calmly. [Theo](https://x.com/theo/status/2125315897854407115): "These guys are so stupid it's insane." Then [a bluff](https://x.com/theo/status/2125316769858683312) (5,642 likes): "Lesson learned. We will close the source of T3 Code tonight. You guys are not responsible with open source software." A [follow-up](https://x.com/theo/status/2125317722552889353) walked it back: "We won't actually do this btw. It would be a massive overreaction. Like the thing you all are doing." [@denispleiades](https://x.com/denispleiades/status/2125326144866234792) was not amused: "Grow some fucking balls and own your mistakes." On the missing toggle, [Theo](https://x.com/theo/status/2125326797915267370) argued "A UI toggle doesn't cover the edge cases u care about, like managed policy or multi environment" and "You can literally just ask T3 Code to disable the telemetry in T3 Code". [@MiaAI_lab](https://x.com/MiaAI_lab/status/2125325380965859548) answered for a lot of people: "No need to remove anything, just give the option to disable it if someone is concerned about their privacy."
- **The fix.** About an hour later [Theo posted the real response](https://x.com/theo/status/2125332390239494211) (6,762 likes, 818K views): "we just merged a change to remove all usage of your Claude and Codex json files to identify unique users". The stated reason for the hashing was deduplication, because user counts were "insanely inflated (like literally 3 million + users if I counted without this)". The post lists what T3 says it never did, including "We NEVER uploaded ANY files from a user's device" and "We NEVER received ANY user data, such as prompts, requests/responses, files, file paths, etc". It also admits the Claude hashes "were broken". The [telemetry doc](https://github.com/pingdotgg/t3code/blob/main/docs/user/telemetry.md) now says product events carry no "identifiers from your provider accounts", and that `T3CODE_TELEMETRY_ENABLED=false` or `DO_NOT_TRACK=1` turns them off.

- **Siren.** [Anthropic](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2125341904644763678) published an eval for sandbox escape: "Across 1,700+ scenarios built to tempt them, recent Claude models accessed, tried to access, or looked outside the sandbox 21-32% of the time, but they rarely misused what they found." ## Models & Research

- **Matrix multiplication at 9/4.** OpenAI's math repository gained a preprint titled [The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/paper.pdf), which hit 527 points on [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50637220). Most people hoped the exponent was 2. As HN's orlp explains, the new paper "proves a lower bound of 2.25" and an earlier preprint in the same repo had the matching upper bound. jcelerier: "This is the exact lower bound (and, sadly > 2)." zone411 counts eight open problems resolved across four new papers, including a strongly polynomial algorithm for linear programming, which is Smale's ninth problem. I have not seen an independent check of any of this. The [README](https://github.com/openai/math) says the manuscripts come from "an unnamed internal OpenAI model", that the catalogue holds "730 manuscripts organized into 375 families" with about 42% of top-line results formalized in Lean, and that they "are best understood as a form of raw research data, rather than polished academic papers".
- **Scott Aaronson pours a glass.** [I've been drinking wine in my kitchen](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10362) ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50637874)) reports that hours after Anthropic's VP≠VNP proof, covered here yesterday, OpenAI released its own. Aaronson says the two proofs look similar and both end in a diagonalization. Then the line that explains the title: "The possibility that this will be followed up soon by a proof of P≠NP itself has not escaped my notice, or anyone else's, certainly not at the AI companies." The post also flags an ECCC preprint from Sanjeev Khanna, Aaron Putterman and Junkai Song with a deterministic linear-time minimum spanning tree algorithm, where the authors write that "the main ideas in [our] paper were entirely generated by AI".
- **Proteins from a general model.** [OpenAI](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2125334383490105601) (8,010 likes, 1.1M views): "Our models can design new protein shapes and sequences using reasoning and code, without calling specialized biology models to generate them." In [the write-up](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-models-demonstrate-intrinsic-protein-design-capabilities/), GPT-6.2 wrote code to produce 3D minibinder designs for five targets, and of 322 tested in the lab with partner Onava, "35 (10.9%) tested positive for binding to their intended target". OpenAI concedes that the original RFdiffusion study reported higher hit rates. Redesigning antibody loops went better: "Of the 182 designs we tested, 128 (70%) showed signs of binding to IL-4Rα."
- **Astra the historian.** Simon Willison retweeted [Mark Humphries](https://x.com/HistoryGPT/status/2125213218045858149) (4,573 likes): "In testing GPT-6.2, I used it to make a significant historical discovery". The model attributed an anonymous sketchbook in Yale's Beinecke Library to the vicomte de Chabot, and Humphries argues it shows research "starting to generalize to non-deterministic fields like history". The [full post](https://generativehistory.substack.com/p/gpt-62-astra-solves-a-historical) has the evidence. [@mdwstfrontierAI](https://x.com/mdwstfrontierAI/status/2125224529589104668) spotted the other thing: "I had to laugh that a chatbot identified de Chabot."
- **OCRia-1 weights.** [Jerry Liu](https://x.com/jerryjliu0/status/2125266115874144473): "Today we are open-sourcing the weights to OCRia-1, our flagship 4B VLM for document OCR 🔥" and "This is our SECOND open-weight release in two days". ## Other Interesting Stuff

- **Abolish lock poisoning.** [Armin Ronacher](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2125365782981062916) wants Rust's mutex poisoning gone, or at least made the exception. From [the post](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/11/25/poisoning/): "I have yet to come across code in the wild that handles a poisoned lock correctly." It collects real failures. A `PoisonError` holds the guard, so an innocent `m.lock()?` can keep a mutex locked. One panic can turn every later `lock().unwrap()` into another panic, and the standard library ignores poisoning in its own locks.
