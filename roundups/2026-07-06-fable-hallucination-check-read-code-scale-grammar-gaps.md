# AI Roundup — July 6, 2026

## Fable 5 Reality Checks

**Matt Pocock: two hallucinations in ten minutes.** [The post (1.5K likes, 136K views)](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073829316555657241): "10 minutes using Fable, 2 factuality-based hallucinations already. Never trust an LLM." The specifics, [per his follow-up](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073830135824875663): misunderstanding the OAuth requirements of X API endpoints, and not knowing the X API got cheaper — "essentially, same mistakes I'd expect from Opus/Sonnet" (context was only [40K tokens](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073865423762739381), so this isn't a rot problem). After a day of "skill issue" drive-bys he [sharpened it](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073866163063275984): "folks who think Fable fixes all hallucinations are talking out of their arse. It's prone to exactly the same failure modes." He also took the maximalist definitional stance — ["If the model answers something wrong, it's a hallucination — whether it's before or after the cutoff"](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073866345616158873) — which drew its own sub-argument. Corroborating reply worth keeping: [Anders Jensen-Waud had Codex fact-check a Fable research pass with browser lookups — 4 of 30 references didn't exist](https://x.com/jensenwaud/status/2073876621019480094). And the "it felt like AGI at launch, now it's Opus-level, they must be throttling it" narrative [is alive in the replies](https://x.com/boni_dev/status/2073900803916497210), a week after the export-control outage made everyone paranoid about silent nerfs.

**Theo pins "You've been lied to about Fable."** [The video post](https://x.com/theo/status/2073866193015087143) is his counter-programming to exactly the discourse above: the claims that Fable is nerfed, over-censored, and terrible at coding. The replies are a neat split-screen of the two realities — [a dev whose years-old tech debt got cleared in a weekend, with Fable writing maestro tests to screenshot an iOS simulator and validate its own work](https://x.com/pbush25/status/2073892695047590059), versus [people citing "an obscene amount" of routine-request refusals on the website](https://x.com/pigeon__s/status/2073870095810699305) and [the note that Anthropic's data-retention policy is blocking enterprise adoption](https://x.com/BenPielstick/status/2073883819904835902). One replier splits the difference cleanly: ["nerfed and terrible at coding don't usually go together — one's a safety tradeoff, the other's a capability claim."](https://x.com/trifon_getsov/status/2073881913547231568) Timing matters: the July 7th subscription cliff is **tomorrow** — Fable comes off Pro/Max plans per Anthropic's capacity plan, and [the replies are counting down the hours](https://x.com/00x1337/status/2073867227703492629).

## The Read-Code Scale

**Matt Pocock turns the binary into a taxonomy.** [The scale post (1.8K likes, 192K views)](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073711736838918436) reframes the week-old "should you read code" fight: (1) read every line of every diff → (2) scan diffs, review important lines → (3) skip diffs but understand the 'why' of every PR → (4) spot-check PRs → (5) skip PRs, spot-check the codebase → (6) ignore code, spot-check *agent traces* to improve the system → (7) ignore everything, models handle it. [His own position](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073712940692242594): "Mostly I'm at 4 and 5, but on higher stakes projects I would go further up the scale, though rarely all the way to 1." Two load-bearing assumptions surface in replies: [he assumes an automated reviewer runs before human review at every level](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073763576074928228), and nearly every serious answer is stakes-conditional — [work 3–4 / personal 5–6](https://x.com/jenks515/status/2073866262279491717), [a team that's lived at 5–6 for months and only drops to 4 "when a trace doesn't make sense and you need the code to explain it"](https://x.com/alexanderbenz/status/2073790185658323421), [prototypes at 7 but "the first exemplar of a pattern" at 1](https://x.com/5imian/status/2073972592692625523).

**Theo's corollary: review less, verify more.** [The post (93K views)](https://x.com/theo/status/2073917360118133191): "You should be reviewing a MUCH smaller % of your code today than you were 5 years ago. If your code is so important it needs every line verified, you better be writing a LOT of slop that verifies it too." [The follow-up](https://x.com/theo/status/2073917650405818584) makes it and-not-or: code important enough for hand review is also important enough for thousands of lines of verification slop. Best concrete framing in the replies is [a selective-review list](https://x.com/MarcosHernanz/status/2073945099558682953) — not reviewing tests, frontend, one-off scripts, isolated services; still reviewing core, database schemas, service contracts — plus [one dev's shift to reviewing "data flow, boundaries/contracts and failure modes" over implementation](https://x.com/toanconquers/status/2073953222419742900). The receipts crowd noticed the drift: ["So we go from 'why are you even reading code?' to 'a % of code needs to be reviewed' in about two days."](https://x.com/swazza85/status/2074021156475044052) His dedicated video on the topic — the one he promised would [piss both sides off](https://x.com/theo/status/2073291309423673462) — is [still in the pipeline](https://x.com/theo/status/2073857738141306940).

## Agentic Coding & Agent Harnesses

**No one matches OpenAI's grammar-constrained tool calls.** Armin Ronacher, straight off last week's Opus-edit-tool failure receipts, [went looking for providers that let you constrain tool-call output with a custom grammar](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2073819360636858450) the way OpenAI does for custom tools. Answer from the thread: nobody. [Hosted APIs mostly stop at JSON Schema for tool calls; arbitrary grammars remain vLLM/Outlines/llama.cpp territory "because custom masks mess with batching and spec decode"](https://x.com/sakurayukiai/status/2073850118269276483), and [flatly: "Anthropic still does not do grammar constrained sampling."](https://x.com/serialx_net/status/2073910373061947502) On the "doesn't it hurt quality?" objection, [Armin's take](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2073851737769689249): true for the small local models he's tested, but "for very large models the story might be different." Also from Armin: pi now has ["robobun at home"](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2073914966936088658) — hosted-style analysis runs that you can then continue as a local pi session, his answer to the analyze-remotely-finish-locally workflow.

**/wayfinder produces its first artifact: a fully-cited PRD.** [Matt Pocock published the PRD from a multi-day /wayfinder session](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073811512938868814) and the pitch is provenance: "every assertion is linked back to the session where it was decided. Secondary source → Primary source." [The workflow](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2073825244347801859): one session builds the initial map, then you attack as many tickets in parallel as possible — "those tickets spawn more tickets, then spawn more tickets, until you resolve all open decisions and you're ready to turn it into a PRD." The interesting pushback (echoed by several users): [wayfinder makes decisions *for* you where /grill-me would have asked first](https://x.com/OmerGronich/status/2073813844137292015) — the orchestrator trades being-in-the-loop for throughput.

**Jerry Liu: file parsing belongs in the agent loop.** [His pitch](https://x.com/jerryjliu0/status/2073863285137342494): agent queries with file attachments are growing exponentially, and the default behavior of every agent loop — a light pypdf/pdftotext pass before falling back to vision — "leads to hallucinated context and bad retrieval," plus wasted turns re-writing import/postprocessing code from scratch. LiteParse is now installable as a one-click skill for any agent, with a template for Vercel's new Eve agent framework. Follows his [Retrieval Harness](https://x.com/jerryjliu0/status/2073407100642852871) from two days ago — LlamaIndex is clearly repositioning its whole stack as agent-loop infrastructure.
