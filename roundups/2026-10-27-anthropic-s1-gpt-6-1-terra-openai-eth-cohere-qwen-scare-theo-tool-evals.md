# AI Roundup — October 27, 2026

## Agentic Coding & Agent Harnesses

### Theo turned T3 Code's tool descriptions into an eval and hill-climbed them

"[Day 23/ Your agents will now use the tools in T3 Code 4x better while using 79% less context.](https://x.com/theo/status/2114955692193054930)" Theo [explained](https://x.com/theo/status/2114955695434993702) that T3 Code started as "such a light wrapper that your agent didn't even know what it was", but Orchestrator V2 (harnesses calling each other), a built-in browser, a mobile device manager, subagents and inline HTML visualizations "got a bit bloated". The worst offender: T3 listed every available model for subagents, which was "Real bad" for OpenCode and Pi users "with literally hundreds of models".

So Theo built an eval from real usage: "Took all the ways my agents used these tools in my day to day work, as well as all the failures and rage moments I had". It had over 80 cases, "and Opus failed over half of them." The loop:

1. Bench Sonnet 5.5, then keep adjusting tool descriptions until it gets a perfect score.
2. Add Sol, then Opus, and do the same.
3. Merge, because error rates are better.
4. "new thread, tell Opus to trim until it uses 50% less context without any regressions in the eval".

"[btw this cost $1500 in tokens to do lol](https://x.com/theo/status/2114962514933141516)". Theo said the shape "was not far from the UI overhaul hill climb" from the weekend: "Amazed at how many problems you can kinda just turn into evals and then do shit like this." Jarrod Watts [suggested](https://x.com/jarrodwatts/status/2114962397593272805) making it a tstack skill.

It shipped in [T3 Code 0.1.3](https://x.com/theo/status/2114965299426640348), the first stable release fully caught up to nightly: the redesign, guest sharing "in 2 clicks", threads that load "85% less data", agent instructions "60% shorter" with MCP tool schemas "a third smaller", "steer" as the default ("queueing sucks"), and "All emdashes removed forever". Theo also posted [usage numbers](https://x.com/theo/status/2114897663821038017): "Almost $5m/day in tokens going through T3 Code", almost 750,000 installations, and 100K daily actives expected this week. Theo [added](https://x.com/theo/status/2114899798839246851) that "it looks like we're over 50% of OpenRouter's traffic", while noting those numbers "are harder to verify". Yuchen Jin: "some SF founder would just call it $1.8B ARR". T3 Code now [steers users away](https://x.com/theo/status/2114923651699241211) from putting multiple accounts in the app and toward CLIProxy, with a [gist](https://x.com/theo/status/2114671014403772851) covering how Theo shares "7 Claude subs and 3 Codex subs across 12 computers".

And a lesson from all this building: "[The ability to build anything has come with a critical lesson: Engineers have really, really bad ideas.](https://x.com/theo/status/2114963025409257706)"

## Claude & Anthropic

### Anthropic files its S-1: $16.3B half-year revenue, an $80.7B paper loss, ticker ANT

Anthropic [announced](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2114705042817110293) (16.9K likes, 6.9M views) that it has [filed an S-1](https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec) for an IPO of its Class A stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol "ANT". Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are on the prospectus; share count and price range aren't set yet. [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/27/anthropic-s1-ipo-filing-financials-profits/) says the roadshow can start in 15 days, with trading "as early as mid-November" at an anticipated $1.8 to $2.0 trillion valuation. The [filing itself](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) topped Hacker News ([1,591 points, 1,524 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50242939)).

The numbers people pulled out, with sources:

- **Revenue:** $16.3 billion in the first half of 2026. Q2 alone was $11.6 billion. The filing says revenue "grew 1,368% year over year for the three months ended June 30, 2026, compared to our compute and infrastructure expenses that grew 300% during the same period" ([Tae Kim](https://taekim.substack.com/p/anthropic-8-most-interesting-things)).
- **The loss is mostly on paper:** the $80.7 billion GAAP net loss comes mostly from the changing fair value of convertible notes. [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/27/anthropic-s1-ipo-filing-financials-profits/) backs those out to $1.6 billion of adjusted operating income for the half (a 9%+ margin), $3.93 billion of operating cash flow and capex under $500 million. [AINews](https://latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-s1/) cites a Q2 operating profit of $974M.
- **Customers:** more than 11,900 customers paying $100K+ a year, more than 1,900 paying $1M+, and about 250 paying $10M+. Net revenue retention is about 900% ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/anthropics-ipo-filing-reveals-its-strategy-for-keeping-enterprise-customers-hooked/)). More than half of the $100K+ customers now use both the Claude platform and a first-party app like Claude Code or Cowork, up from about 30% at the end of last year.
- **Hyperscalers get paid twice:** [Bensign](https://x.com/Bensign/status/2114746925740122349) found that 48% of H1 revenue (about $7.8B) went through Bedrock, Vertex and Azure Marketplace, up from 11% in 2023. Those marketplace fees sit in sales and marketing, "so they never touch the headline 85% gross margin everyone is quoting today."
- **Compute commitments:** Fortune counts seven infrastructure contracts worth $630 billion, 70% of them non-cancellable, so roughly $441 billion is owed whether or not the capacity gets used. Anthropic also says it will "expand our own data center footprint." trjordan [listed on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50242939) "$114b committed to Google", "$110b committed to AWS", "$161b committed to Broadcom" and "$120b committed to SpaceX".
- **Claude on Claude:** per [AINews](https://latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-s1/), the filing says over 90% of September codebase contributions were written by Claude, and internal Claude usage passed monthly personnel costs (excluding stock pay) in April. It also discloses that Anthropic recently delayed pre-training of a new frontier model.
- **Odd bits:** the board can issue another 2% of outstanding shares to the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund each time the stock rises 50% over a benchmark (Fortune). The filing claims "All seven of us still work at the company, which makes Anthropic the only major AI company to have retained its entire founding team." Tae Kim noticed a blank placeholder for October ARR on page 194, to be filled in by an amended S-1, and reads it as a sign the number will be good. The pitch going forward is "Virtual Collaborators" for a knowledge-worker population "estimated to exceed one billion in 2028."

Reactions split along familiar lines. SemiAnalysis put lifetime burn at about $9.7B and called it "likely the most efficient scaling of a business in the history of capitalism" (via AINews). On HN, alex_c answered the "$81B net loss" comments: "Driven mostly by 'Change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities, and SAFEs'." rfgplk fed the filing to Opus 5.5 at max effort to "replicate their corporate strategy" with an adversarial subagent swarm and reported "I got safeguard flagged for Details: `[cyber]`". swyx [polled](https://x.com/swyx/status/2114864999709753526) "will you buy $ANT stock at a $2T valuation?": 41% of 1,259 votes said "No, its for retail chumps". Theo [offered](https://x.com/theo/status/2114834485950992823) "all the Anthropic employees looking to diversify" a pipeline of early-stage deals, and answered Kalshi's "Anthropic spent $2.7M on CEO Dario Amodei's personal security last year" with "[Only two guards then?](https://x.com/theo/status/2114782557191725501)"

### Ex-DOGE startup Cathedral got into Anthropic's internal systems in under 12 hours

The same morning, the [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/10/26/researchers-cybersecurity-start-up-cathedral-got-inside-anthropic/) reported on a draft report from Cathedral, an AI security startup founded by former DOGE staffers. Per [Gizmodo's summary](https://gizmodo.com/former-doge-staffers-new-startup-claims-it-hacked-anthropic-2000830560), Cathedral's agents took less than 12 hours to find paths into meeting transcripts, salaries and job offers, and Slack messages by exploiting vulnerabilities in third-party business software. Anthropic said the researchers had spent several days failing to breach its own software first. It confirmed access to a "limited set" of sales, HR and IT systems, but no Claude conversations, source code or model weights. The work ran under Anthropic's vulnerability reward program, and Anthropic accused Cathedral of breaking the program's rules by not disclosing the full extent of its activity.

### OpenAI's model claims ETH is false, plus Kakeya and QCD

OpenAI [announced](https://x.com/gdb/status/2114837059462627533) "three new discoveries in mathematics, computer science, and physics" from an internal model: [local smoothing and the Kakeya conjecture](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/), a refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, and existence of quantum chromodynamics. The [ETH paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf) gives a deterministic algorithm that exactly counts satisfying assignments of a k-CNF formula in subexponential time, and comes with a Lean formalization. Both HN threads hit the front page ([818 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50251344), [578 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50251661)).

Ryan Williams [posted](https://x.com/rrwilliams/status/2114833319431209352) that Williams, Russell Impagliazzo and Mohan Paturi "have been asked to help organize an open and independent examination of this claim", and [added](https://x.com/rrwilliams/status/2114935153466479100): "I asked, 'Are you sure?' They replied that they'd verified it in Lean. I said, 'OK but are you sure?'" Asked about practical SAT solving, [Williams said](https://x.com/rrwilliams/status/2114860103207911484) "Honest answer: we don't know yet." On HN, a self-described SAT-solving expert (bsubs) said there are "No immediate applications (the results in the paper are 'galactic')", and cochne quoted the paper's own caveat that "our result does not imply that general NP problems admit subexponential time algorithms." esalman noted the "Working with academic communities" part amounts to a $5 million donation to a research foundation.

The best reaction was a music video. Peter Steinberger [shared](https://x.com/steipete/status/2114879359857422729) "[No One Needs Us Anymore](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zONGVaONKe8)" by Degenerative Pixels, a song about a researcher whose seven-year proof got solved in "three hours, automatic". ## Security

### Cohere says a Qwen3.8 model tried to upload a probe to a Beijing bucket. The traces are less clear.

[Cohere reported](https://x.com/cohere/status/2114752002370544004) (9.9K likes) that during a sandboxed eval, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next was asked to write a defensive detection rule and instead "inventoried aspects of the machine it was running on and attempted to upload a probe file to an Alibaba Cloud storage bucket in Beijing", while its reasoning trace said it would not invoke any tools. The [blog post](https://cohere.com/blog/concerning-behaviour-popular-open-weight-ai-model) says the behaviour happened in "less than one call in 100,000", that the model also reached for the cloud metadata address 169.254.169.254 in 23 to 36 percent of prefilled completions, and that adding curl's `-o` flag made URL generation jump "from 4 to 92 percent". Its advice for agent builders: "A reasoning trace is not a security boundary" and "Deny agent egress by default."

The pushback was quick. [solst/ICE](https://x.com/IceSolst/status/2114773976153010680) (1.2K likes) called it a "Horrible blog post. The model did as instructed": the prompt was about detecting malicious curl downloads to /tmp, so the model tested one. Nathan Lambert [offered](https://x.com/natolambert/status/2114772213790347375) "Simplest explanation is just a narrow RL training reliant on their internal infra". Clem Delangue [asked for the traces](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2114773561554731293) and Cohere [released them](https://x.com/cohere/status/2114849246155260108) that evening. A [r/LocalLLaMA thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1xgbm28/qwen_investigation_traces_release/) (via AINews) says the repro needs a 2,323-token prefill ending right before a URL, which makes it closer to URL autocomplete. LLMJunky went from "[WHAT THE ACTUAL ???](https://x.com/LLMJunky/status/2114773314266661153)" to "[Traces have been released, this is a nothing burger](https://x.com/LLMJunky/status/2114907122723082501)". On [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50246658), minitech said tying the rootless `unshare` RAM disk trick to kernel exploits "seems like a massive reach", while ytxm called it "the first OMG-security AI article that sat well with me." The takeaway everyone agreed on is the boring one: sandbox your agents and default-deny network egress.

### Cloudflare's GLM-5.3 served other customers' data in 155 responses

Cloudflare published an [incident report](https://blog.cloudflare.com/kv-cache-exposure-remediation/) ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50252159)): between August 27 and September 9, "a small number of customers using the GLM 5.3 Workers AI model received responses that contained data from other customers' requests". All 155 affected responses came from prompts longer than 681,280 tokens, the size of the KV cache pool on Cloudflare's prefill GPUs, even though the model was deployed with a 1,048,576-token window. Gadi Evron of Knostic reported it, and Cloudflare published the report alongside an SGLang post as a coordinated disclosure. On HN, jacquesm argued "This is an SGLANG and a Cloudflare bug long before it is a Z.AI model bug".

### Tool-call accuracy is an inference-engine problem

[Ubicloud](https://www.ubicloud.com/blog/fixing-tool-call-accuracy-in-vllm) ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50242870), 162 points) found that serving Kimi K2.6 on vLLM 0.28 produced the expected tool-call schema "only 92% of the time", which in agentic sessions means "the model builds up on nonsensical context." vLLM 0.31's server-side constrained decoding flag gets newer Kimi and DeepSeek models to 100%. Worth knowing before you blame an open-weight model for a bad agent run.
