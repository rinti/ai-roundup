# AI Roundup — September 27, 2026

The big political headline this week is the "Super Intelligence" rebrand. The big developer headline is Anthropic opening the plugin submission portal. And the most resonant take belongs to Simon Willison: coding agents make software engineering *even harder*.

## AI Industry & Policy

### Trump and Xi agree to rename AI "Super Intelligence"

On September 25, President Trump announced that he and China's President Xi Jinping agreed to rename Artificial Intelligence to "Super Intelligence." Trump posted on Truth Social that Xi "seemed to like calling the poorly and inaccurately named Artificial Intelligence to a far more accurate and important name, SUPER INTELLIGENCE." The State Department has been instructed to rebrand AI under the new banner across all US government documents. ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/09/25/trumps-newest-rebranding-project-technology-formerly-known-ai/), [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/6104142-trump-renames-ai-super-intelligence/), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/trump-gets-xi-to-agree-to-call-ai-super-intelligence-white-house-says-2000817802))

Worth noting: Xi addressed AI publicly at the White House but notably did not use the term "superintelligence." Trump had earlier run a Truth Social poll offering options like "Extreme Intelligence" and "Supreme Intelligence." The tech industry is less than thrilled — "superintelligence" already has a very specific meaning in AI safety discourse.

[Armin Ronacher](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2103485472278454438) (@mitsuhiko) responded: **"I'm really curious who in our industry will embrace the Super Intelligence thing."** A question that captures the mood perfectly.

### Dario Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" continues to ripple

Dario Amodei's September 12 essay arguing that frontier AI labs must slow down and allow third-party evaluators permanent, employee-level system access continues to generate discussion. Sam Altman, Elon Musk, and Demis Hassabis all endorsed the proposal. [Andrej Karpathy](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2102063518979969034) quipped: **"So ready to do my part in pacing the frontier 🫡 :D"** — notable given he joined Anthropic's pretraining team earlier this year. ([Zvi Mowshowitz analysis](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/we-must-pace-the-frontier), [ComputingForGeeks explainer](https://computingforgeeks.com/we-must-pace-the-frontier-explained/))

## Claude Code & Anthropic Updates

### Anthropic opens the Claude plugin directory submission portal

On September 25, Anthropic launched a [directory submission portal](https://claude.com/blog/build-plugins-for-claude) for Claude plugins — packages of MCP connectors, Agent Skills, or both. Developers on paid Claude plans can now submit plugins, track review status, and see usage analytics. Two submission paths: a single MCP connector pointing to a remote server, or a GitHub-hosted plugin bundle combining MCP servers and skills. The official marketplace has crossed 200 plugins. MCP usage across Claude products is **up 110x this year**. ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-opens-directory-submission-portal-for-claude-plugins/))

[Boris Cherny](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2103691327699550598) (@bcherny), head of Claude Code, responded to the announcement: **"Can't wait to see what you build."**

### Claude Code v2.1.283

September 26 brought Claude Code v2.1.283 with LLM gateway request grouping (`x-claude-code-prompt-id`) and a new `availableModelsMatch` setting to control model version access. Activity remained steady with 18 new issues on September 27, including reports about MCP validation strictness and non-ASCII file path handling. ([Releasebot changelog](https://releasebot.io/updates/anthropic/claude-code))

## Agentic Coding & Agent Harnesses

### Simon Willison: "Coding agents make software engineering even harder"

On September 24, [Simon Willison](https://x.com/simonw/status/2103288927805476900) dropped the take of the week: **"The more time I spend working with coding agents, the more convinced I am that they make software engineering even harder. We can do amazing things with them, but unlocking their full potential requires extraordinary discipline and knowledge."** ([Note on simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/24/harder/))

This landed the same week he closed the [WeAreDevelopers World Congress North America](https://www.wearedevelopers.com/world-congress-north-america/agenda) keynote (September 23–25, San José) with a pixel-art kākāpō party — two dozen bouncing kākāpō under confetti, generated entirely by Claude Opus 5.5 as a single 45KB HTML file with no images, no video, and no network calls. The tool is live at [tools.simonwillison.net/kakapo-party](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/26/kakapo-party/) with the full transcript and Playwright script published alongside.

### Matt Pocock ships "Software Factory" to the AI Coding Dictionary

On September 24, [Matt Pocock](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2103103504567107712) shipped a new entry to the [AI Coding Dictionary](https://github.com/mattpocock/dictionary-of-ai-coding): **Software Factory** — "the hype term of the day." A software factory is a system where coding-agent sessions are started by triggers (issue creation, cron schedules, CI failures, monitoring alerts) rather than by a person. Without a factory, every agent session still needs a human to open it. A "dark factory" entry was added the same day. The dictionary is open-source on GitHub.

On September 25, Pocock also [tweeted](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2103590750617485505): **"Watched a @poteto video this morning. Shipped more work than I have done in weeks. Sometimes correlation is causation."**

The @poteto video Pocock referenced is Lauren Tan's (SpaceXAI) **"how I shipped 2,500 PRs last month to production"** — a 38-minute talk originally meant for Cursor Compile in London, released free on X. It describes pstack, her MIT-licensed Cursor plugin for verified agent work. The PR volume is self-reported and unaudited, but the workflow is detailed and the video is generating significant discussion about scaling AI-assisted development. ([folkfox analysis](https://folkfox.com/pstack-2500-ai-agent-prs-verification/), [X post](https://x.com/poteto/status/2102050467505430555))

The gap is closing and the cost-performance tradeoff is the real differentiator now.

## Model Releases & Analysis

### Simon Willison on Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol & Luna

Simon Willison's September 22 blog post remains the best overview of the week's model releases. Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 — first in the 5.5 generation, with a 40% price cut vs. Opus 5 ($4/$20 per million tokens, 1M context, 128K max output). About an hour later, OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Sol and its budget sibling GPT-6 Luna ($2/$10 per million tokens). Willison didn't pick a winner — he used Sol for one job, Opus 5.5 for another, and Luna for a third. ([Blog post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/22/opus-and-sol-and-luna/), [X post](https://x.com/simonw/status/2102546103984079131))

### Jev: the "System One" decision model

On September 21, TypeSafe AI launched Jev — a model that accepts text input but returns floating-point numbers (categories, yes/no, ratings, confidence scores) instead of text. Simon Willison [covered it](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/21/jev/) as "a new shape of LLM." The idea: split decision-making from generation. Jev runs at 70–500ms latency (usually ~100ms), scored 95.9% on PriorBench classification vs. 77.2% for keyword rules, and never hallucinates because it doesn't generate text. Think of it as a smarter if-else that makes decisions fast and typed, while the LLM handles the thinking. ([Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/jev-vs-llms-when-ai-moves-from-generation-to-decision-making/), [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/system-one-models-jev))
