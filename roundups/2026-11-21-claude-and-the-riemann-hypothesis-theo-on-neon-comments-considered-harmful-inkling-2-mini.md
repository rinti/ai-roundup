# AI Roundup — November 21, 2026

## Claude and the Riemann Hypothesis

**The claim.** On Friday, Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge posted [a short note](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) that says: "I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis" (quoted by [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/anthropic-mathematician-claims-to-prove-riemann-hypothesis-most-famous-problem-in-all-of-math/)). The result is the *Generalized* Riemann Hypothesis, and the note links a Lean formalization. Scott Aaronson [put its size](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10344) at "17.4 million (!!) lines of Lean code". MIT's Andrew Sutherland, who had independently compiled the Lean result, told SciAm: "This is indeed an historic moment... The world has changed." The note itself sounds mournful: "It is natural to feel that this is an end, in my case a loss of a principal guiding light". The SciAm tweet [got 16,600 likes and 3.7M views](https://x.com/sciam/status/2123782587235655707), and the top reply, at 3,900 likes, was the joke "Prompt: Solve the Riemann hypothesis, make no mistakes and no em dashes".

**HN's reaction** ([2,629 points, 1,800 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50574968)). Commenters went over the note's own caveat that the argument is "incredibly long at the moment" and that the formalization would be "a huge, unreadable document". [puff_pastry](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50575311) worried that "we're back in the world of 'magic' and 'belief'". Others pointed at the note's line that Claude "flew, and danced, and sparkled near that" finish. Simon Willison [added context](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50575448): Alpöge was one of the two mathematicians working on Navier–Stokes when OpenAI rushed out its own result in September.

**A human proof, announced in a hurry.** Hours later, Ravi Vakil [posted](https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/) from an AMS board meeting that Alessandro Maria Masullo has a proposed proof of GRH. It is "all human, with no AI", and Brian Conrad "has been looking closely at it for months". The plan had been a year of polishing, but "Now it seems best to put out the current draft." About eight hours later Vakil added an update: "Several people have contacted me to report problems in the proposed proof, identified with the assistance of different AI models." The [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50579568) (2,045 points) became an argument about priority. [A_D_E_P_T](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50579895) warned that if you sit on a long project, "an Anthropic or OpenAI employee is going to solve your problem when they decide to bother with it". [yewenjie](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50580015) replied that mathematicians don't want to look ridiculous on something as big as RH, and pointed to the Mochizuki/ABC fiasco.

**Tao's tally.** Terry Tao [listed three results](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/unforced-euler-singularity-unforced-navier-stokes-singularity-and-the-generalized-riemann-hypothesis/) "in decreasing order of how 'digested'" they are:

- A human-readable proof of finite-time blowup for the unforced 3D Euler equations by Alpöge, Buckmaster and Coiculescu, "with heavy AI assistance", [formalized in Lean](https://github.com/mcoiculescu/EulerBlowupPublic).
- A Navier–Stokes blowup proof by Alpöge and Buckmaster with a [Lean formalization](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/). It is technically readable, but Tao says it still needs "a proper, professionally written proof" (HN: [286 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50576232)).
- The GRH proof: "(enormous) Lean-only", generated "using an internal AI model". Tao says the strategy appears to use "the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry" and points readers to the Lean Zulip thread.

**Aaronson's bet.** In "[The stochastic parrots have apparently proved the Riemann Hypothesis](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10344)", Aaronson grants that Lean has kernel bugs that have "in the past, allowed proofs of 0=1". Still, "not one of the AI results with Lean certificates has needed to be retracted", so "my bet is that the proof will stand." Mathematicians "won't be satisfied with 17 million lines of Lean" until they can "explain and distill and reconstruct the ideas for themselves", and Aaronson expects that to take years.

**The verifiability take.** Jerry Liu [rewrote](https://x.com/jerryjliu0/status/2123969065836490973) Sigal Samuel's "math is a highly verifiable task so you should *expect* this breakthrough" as "why's everyone so shocked about curing cancer? ... the patient either has cancer or they don't" (586 likes). Replies argued both ways: Lean can check math, but nobody can emulate clinical trials. Lee Robinson [summed up the arc](https://x.com/leerob/status/2123845449413239273): "What happened to software, then happened to math, and now is happening to almost every job that uses a computer. The models are already smart enough. Now the products must follow." In the same thread Lee linked a [Stanford CS146S lecture on always-on agents](https://x.com/leerob/status/2123854868637204964). Other HN essays: [Re: Riemann](https://borretti.me/article/re-riemann) (198 points).

**Markets.** It was also Anthropic's second trading day. "[Nasdaq erases all 2026 gains as AI sell-off dampens Anthropic's second day](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50574383)" (Guardian, 317 points, 576 comments). Most of the HN thread asked why OpenAI didn't IPO first.

## Agentic Coding & Agent Harnesses

- **Matt Pocock: "comments considered harmful".** [Prompt of the day](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2123798388227403983) (3,700 likes): have the agent sort every code comment into four kinds and act on each:
  - *Tautology* or *Stale*: delete it.
  - *Excuse* ("many comments allow the agent to get away with a hacky implementation, which then gets propagated through the code"): delete it and file an issue.
  - *Decision Record*: move it into an ADR.

  Then add a lint rule banning comments, taking "a ratchet approach", and create a `/delete-comment` skill to repeat it later. Pushback in the replies: [@f_stack_snack](https://x.com/f_stack_snack/status/2124060296419397905) asked why you'd move the "why" away from the code "and pray that an agent editing this piece of code would find relevant ADR". [Matt's answer](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2124060786641313884): "If your domain language is set up right, i.e. things are named the same, then relevant ADR's are going to be shown whenever it searches for the right terms". [@harrisonju](https://x.com/harrisonju/status/2123801475092529449) warned that "the only place left for an excuse to live is an ADR dressed up as a decision."
- **Matt's /vector skill for non-devs.** Matt [is "cooking"](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2123810087361810663) a skill that guides people through delegating any task to AI in six steps: find workflows, give agents context, reorder the workflow, and add memory so it improves over time. Docs and frameworks for company "VECTOR sessions" are coming (1,800 likes, 136 replies, mostly non-devs saying yes).
- **Codex's model WebSocket gets 8x bigger.** Charlie Marsh [explains](https://x.com/charliermarsh/status/2123849871480463669) that Codex sends model requests over a WebSocket so data goes out incrementally. The size limit went from 16 to 128 MB, "so large turns avoid falling back to HTTP (bad!)", and over-limit requests are now ~5x faster. Tibo: "[Actual engineering going on here.](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2123859787079426435)" One reply [reported](https://x.com/farovitus/status/2123865016583798966) that launch data usage dropped "from ~500 MB to just 14.5 MB".
- **Theo wants ACLs in git.** "[Source control being 100% or 0% access feels stupider than ever](https://x.com/theo/status/2123901093751280052)": Theo wants private files, private branches "that only 3 team members can see", and built-in secret management (3,500 likes, 280 replies). [@HSVSphere](https://x.com/HSVSphere/status/2123903161358336391) sketched per-revision ACLs on top of jj. [@tofuuuuh](https://x.com/tofuuuuh/status/2123916623555997990) put the agent angle well: "'can you fix this button?' shouldn't also mean 'here are the production keys'". In a separate post Theo called an annotated screenshot "[a golden example](https://x.com/theo/status/2123753984569864630)" of feedback: "I'll take a pic like that over a PR literally any day".

## Models & Research

- **Inkling 2 Mini** ([HN, 977 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50581260)). Thinking Machines' [new open model](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/) is "a looped transformer, running the middle layers of the model three times for every token". It is an MoE with 26.4B total and 3.4B active parameters, mostly trained and deployed in NVFP4, with a ~20GB footprint. It leads open models under 100B on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%). The team also released a variable-loop research variant: its CoT stayed legible as loops increased, and sabotage tests didn't trend higher. Unsloth GGUFs and a llama.cpp looped-architecture PR appeared within hours.
- **Frostbite** ([HN, 645 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50572503)). Architect Labs [says](https://architectlabs.com/blog/frostbite) its AI systems "autonomously iterated over the compiler, ISA, architecture, microarchitecture, RTL, verification, and final GDS" for a new CPU built on interaction nets. It claims 4.59x perf/W against a silicon-proven dual-issue in-order core across 25 workloads, 15 of them held out.
- **"Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights"** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885), [HN 413 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50577265)). The paper describes a model updating its own weights through its own reasoning, which the authors call a step toward recursive self-improvement. Top HN complaint: "Alignment" is not mentioned once.
- GPT-6.2 Astra [scored 41.2% on the Ember-0 robotics benchmark](https://x.com/xdofai/status/2123822009696342190), up from GPT-6 Astra's 19.6% in early September (RT by OpenAIDevs).

## Security & Privacy

- **2M canary prompts.** Dragos (HD Moore's team) [sent nearly two million prompts](https://www.dragos.com/blog/ai-supply-chain-security-canary-tokens) with unique URLs and canary AWS credentials to AI services between July and November. They found evidence that "at least three AI providers" were compromised, marketplace resellers retaining prompts "using standard logging capabilities", and providers ignoring ZDR. In two of the breaches, "some of the stolen requests were marked as ZDR." thdxr [cited it](https://x.com/thdxr/status/2123782259916349514) as "another reason why we're moving to get 100% of our inference done on our own GPUs".
- **Signal blocks OS agents.** Signal [will block screenshots](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/) in its app on iOS 27, and on Android when an AI assistant tries to capture the screen. The goal is to keep Siri AI's Onscreen Awareness and Gemini Intelligence from capturing messages, as Signal did with Windows Recall ([HN, 550 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50572348)).
