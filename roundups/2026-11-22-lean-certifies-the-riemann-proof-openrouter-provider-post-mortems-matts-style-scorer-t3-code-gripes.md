# AI Roundup — November 22, 2026

## The Riemann Proof, Certified

- **The joint statement.** The Lean and Mathlib communities [published a statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/), endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative: "We hereby certify that both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct." The rationale: the 17-million-line library depends only on Mathlib, and the statement it proves is Mathlib's own `RiemannHypothesis`. Dozens of people compiled it and reproduced the same raw export. Two independent tools confirmed that the theorem matches Mathlib's statement exactly. The export passed Lean's kernel and several independent checkers. Two of them, con-leche and con-ron, come with a formal proof, relative to set theory, that they "cannot accept a false proof". The checks ran on "an inert export of the proof, out of reach of hypothetical malicious code in the Lean proof sources." It closes: "We look forward to all the activity in the mathematical community that will help explain the new ideas in this proof." (HN: [1,028 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50589618).)
- **The verification log.** Andrew Sutherland's [MIT verification page](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) ("checks run with Claude Code") lists every step. All 175,187 modules compile on Lean/Mathlib v4.33.1. The five headline theorems use only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. A sandboxed rebuild with no network reproduces the 61.6 GB export byte for byte. The independent Rust kernel nanoda accepted the full export in 27 minutes on 80 threads. As of 04:35 UTC today, the Lean default and paranoid kernel runs on the export were still going.
- **Andrew Curran's thread.** [@AndrewCurran_](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2124178939287687297) posted the statement (2,800 likes). [@mathepi](https://x.com/mathepi/status/2124216097272742131) asked "have they really looked thru 17 M lines of lean, looking for all the little gotchas?", and Curran [answered](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2124217097115045995): "Like in everything else, we will increasingly rely on models to verify models work." Another [reply from Curran](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2124197185587995022): "I can't see six months out anymore, and I was really good at it." [@yevloop](https://x.com/yevloop/status/2124181834590478341): "lean signing off kills like half the takes from yesterday."
- **Thariq's cyborgs.** [Thariq (@trq212)](https://x.com/trq212/status/2124239682691387634) (2,000 likes): watching @__alpoge__ on the Riemann Hypothesis or @jarredsumner on ahead-of-time JavaScript, "I feel like I'm seeing cyborgs. humans and machines working in beautiful tandem, pushing the frontier together." In a [follow-up](https://x.com/trq212/status/2124239686482923553): "as models get more powerful, the skill of working with them becomes more and more important, and I have not seen diminishing returns on that skill yet". [Another](https://x.com/trq212/status/2124239688013914132): "I fear we are too quick to disintermediate ourselves, and by doing so we do both ourselves and the models disservice." The pushback was about access. [@promptprxncess](https://x.com/promptprxncess/status/2124245380946608341) quoted a post about discoveries "made using models that the rest of us plebs aren't even allowed to know the names of". [@codewithaltin](https://x.com/codewithaltin/status/2124252107033157653): "the model makes experts way faster, it doesn't skip the part where you can tell a right answer from a plausible one."
- **What's next?** On HN, the top threads asked what humans can learn from a proof nobody has read. One commenter [noted](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50590054) that the many results conditional on RH now hold. Another [expects](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50591946) "a lot to be studied", since the proof "contains about all of the number theory produced in the last 50 years".

## Security & Privacy

The canary-prompt study from yesterday's issue named names this weekend.

- **The viral summary.** [@michael_chomsky](https://x.com/michael_chomsky/status/2124008002571428224) (2,200 likes, 364K views): a researcher sent secret links, "40 to 130 hours later someone opened the secret links", and "AltasCloud/Novita/StreamLake were forwarding requests to MiniMax". [swyx asked](https://x.com/swyx/status/2124147427313861009) "is there any possible benign explanation why these providers were secretly phoning home?" [@rasbt](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2124150661524705393) offered one: cheap providers at capacity "reroute traffic to the 'official' model provider" instead of erroring out. [@yagcidev](https://x.com/yagcidev/status/2124159975492604411) gave the most benign version: "some crawler pulling urls out of logged traffic days later. which still means somebody was logging the traffic". [@ares_mheinke](https://x.com/ares_mheinke/status/2124155441337033131) called it an undisclosed sub-processor under GDPR Art. 28.
- **OpenRouter's response.** [OpenRouter's post](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/) says the findings "did not show any breach or unauthorized access of OpenRouter". It has off-boarded three providers, removed 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention eligibility (some only as a precaution) and notified affected customers. Next, it will run the researchers' canary tests continuously and verify network paths to catch forwarding to third parties.
- **Novita.** [Novita's notice](https://novita.ai/blog/minimax-requests-privacy-notice/): "A routing configuration issue caused an API forwarding path intended for specific customers to apply more broadly." MiniMax requests from 2026-05-28 to 2026-11-11, direct or via OpenRouter, "may have been accessed or retained by a third party". The researcher's URLs were opened, but the canary AWS keys were never used, and "The access patterns suggest automated content review may have been involved."
- **Together AI: the agent committed the key.** [Together's CEO Vipul Ved Prakash writes](https://www.together.ai/blog/security-event-prompt-leakage-for-requests-in-june-and-july-of-2026) that Together stored prompt samples for testing in a private Hugging Face repo. The samples came from MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6 requests in June and July. Then, "During development of our public GitHub repository, xorl, an AI coding agent committed a credential that provided access to the private Hugging Face repository." Scanning caught it and the commit was reverted. In that window, an attacker downloaded the samples and tested any credentials found in them. Five of those were Dragos canaries. The fixes: scoped or short-lived keys, credential checks on every PR, ZDR on by default for new accounts, and "Updating our development processes governing the use of AI coding agents."
- **Phala.** [Phala's preliminary report](https://phala.com/posts/phala-november-preliminary-incident-report): an attacker chained "a legacy internet-facing service with management interfaces that relied on loopback access for trust". They then took over an inference VM on a new GPU server "serving production traffic without our full production security hardening". Contents of DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 requests routed by OpenRouter between Oct 21 and Nov 8 should be treated as exposed, and any secrets in them rotated.

## Agentic Coding & Agent Harnesses

- **Matt's style scorer.** [Matt Pocock](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2124214098984370645) (1,700 likes, 139 replies) described "a new, very simple way of getting an agent to write in your style". You build a "'scorer' app that lets the agent send you example sentences which you score, one at a time, swipe-left style". Then you write an article together sentence by sentence, while the agent "is able to test its own hypotheses about your writing style, and builds up a profile over time." When [@gtanmay442](https://x.com/gtanmay442/status/2124214648744693972) called it "RLHF basically but distilling all that in skills and the agents context", Matt [agreed](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2124214975661047991).
