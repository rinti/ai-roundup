# AI Roundup — November 23, 2026

## OpenAI Publishes Its Perplexity Curve

- **The post.** [Jakub Pachocki (@merettm)](https://x.com/merettm/status/2124549218995949967) (11,600 likes, 8.2M views): "We decided to provide more transparency into pretraining progress at OpenAI for the sake of moving toward shared safety bars." In "[Perplexity and Intelligence](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/)", OpenAI's chief scientist plots log-perplexity on a held-out set for every OpenAI model since GPT-3.5. Some milestones from the post: GPT-3.5 at about 0.99 "was capable of fluent conversation", GPT-4 at about 0.91 was "proficient at utilizing chain-of-thought reasoning", and below 0.8 came "transformative capabilities in fields like cybersecurity and mathematics". On math: "Once we upgraded to a model of log-perplexity ~0.76, it started producing groundbreaking results." The post expects "further transformative capabilities as we enter the ~0.68-0.7 log-perplexity window" and says pretraining research could see "a similar avalanche" of AI-driven advances "as soon as 2027".
- **The proposal.** Pachocki proposes "common limits, based on perplexity and other measurements, that developers are committed to not crossing until our ability to understand and monitor AI behavior achieves a certain concrete milestone (such as the development of a successor to CoT monitors)." [Sam Altman](https://x.com/sama/status/2124551883130114403) quoted that passage (1,600 likes). The post lists its own caveats: different labs use different datasets, perplexity is detached from real tasks, chain-of-thought gains do not show up in it, and nobody knows the floor. A footnote says the held-out data "are sourced from our internal codebase". Another says the 2026 acceleration on the curve came "mostly" from long-term research and compute, "and only in a smaller part to automation".
- **Microsoft reports in.** [Mustafa Suleyman](https://x.com/mustafasuleyman/status/2124663594449318173) (1,350 likes, 4.1M views): "With OpenAI's permission, we report MAI-2 Base on their evaluation set. Its log-perplexity is 0.819, putting it in the same ballpark as the GPT-5.6 Sol base model." MAI-2 uses OpenAI's tokenizer, so the numbers compare directly. Suleyman added that cross-lab comparisons will need bits-per-byte "since different labs use different tokenizers". [@WenhuChen](https://x.com/WenhuChen/status/2124719374779224284) asked how to access the held-out set, and [@Jianyuandeng](https://x.com/Jianyuandeng/status/2124665479167959546) predicted "six months from now the headline is whichever lab quietly stops publishing its number".
- **swyx's A plot.** [swyx](https://x.com/swyx/status/2124583459356373052): "this chart is one of the most beautiful ways to tell the story of the last 4 years. it also shows that a lot of the post training issues we debate is kinda the B plot to the overall A plot of large scale pretrain progress." swyx also [asked](https://x.com/swyx/status/2124590240539259381) whether the metric means "we have seen more ai progress this year than in 2022-2025". [elie (@eliebakouch)](https://x.com/eliebakouch/status/2124588823472648370) warned that "data improvement can 'benchmaxx' perplexity results". Replies kept asking [who picks the text](https://x.com/lozoboss/status/2124593398447558833), because "that choice ends up being the whole rule".
- **The replies under Pachocki.** [Daniel Kokotajlo](https://x.com/DKokotajlo/status/2124561805167284712): "Thank you for sharing this info. More than Anthropic did!" (753 likes). [@pr337h4m](https://x.com/pr337h4m/status/2124552120955539482): "funny that y'all are posting this exactly when you're perceived as losing" (514). [Dimitris Papailiopoulos](https://x.com/DimitrisPapail/status/2124566074440171776) asked for "a public Val set that would be to some degree representative", [rohan anil](https://x.com/_arohan_/status/2124567101977608351) wanted GLM and Kimi on the chart, and [Sebastian Raschka](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2124626470014464238) noted "this is just pre-training, not even considering harness progress!" The top reply, from [Elon Musk](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2124588982117781808) (6,700 likes), said "Pretraining should just be called 'training'".
- **HN (563 points).** [amluto](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50601957) asked why next-token prediction on OpenAI's internal codebase should track overall capability. An OpenAI employee, [tedsanders](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50602183), answered: "The correlation between perplexity and the quality isn't 1, but neither is it 0." ## The Riemann Proofs, Day Three

- **Baez on Masullo.** Two attempts appeared on November 20: Levent Alpöge's, which "exists mainly as 17 million lines of Lean code", and Alessandro Masullo's, "a 940-page human-written manuscript" that Ravi Vakil posted. [John Baez sketched Masullo's strategy](https://johncarlosbaez.wordpress.com/2026/11/22/masullo-on-the-riemann-hypothesis/) ([HN, 191 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50598441)). The idea is a new trace formula for the zeta function's logarithmic derivative that visibly has no poles off the critical line. Baez then lists the problems reviewers found "within hours". [Eric Naslund's AI-assisted review](https://github.com/enaslund/rh-verification) argues that a limit predicted to be log 2 is actually 0. A checking effort flagged a page-444 claim about Markushevich bases that "apparently" fails. Chapter 13, where GRH should be deduced, "contains only placeholders". In a comment, Baez quotes Masullo's statement: "The paper is in no way in a state that is fit for submission or dissemination."
- **The agents formalize it.** Ryan McCorvie is running a crowdsourced "[forensic formalization](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo)" in Lean, where "each statement will be formalized then proved, refuted or left as a gap", and asked people to donate tokens. Its [progress map](https://claude.ai/artifact/CtQyX8qfYAF822bgHJHHE3) is a Claude artifact ([HN, 219 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50605045)). Baez relays a Lean Zulip note from Richard Osborn: "when some agents get stuck, they start trying to switch over to using the Alpöge/Claude proof!" On HN, [hodgehog11](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50605316) warned against reading the progress bar as a sign the proof holds, "as it takes only one hole for the entire thing to come crashing down". [medler](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50605370) added that the bar counts issues filed, with no idea how many remain.
- **The credit fight.** The top HN comment on the Baez post ([davesque](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50606852)) worries that "12 years of this man's life have been cheapened by this whole ordeal". [ComplexSystems](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50607154) replied: "Who gives a crap if Alpöge was first™ by a few hours? Give them both credit". [glerk](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50607226) pointed out that Masullo's approach is "completely different" from Alpöge's "and not at all redundant".
- **Next up, P vs NP.** [pequalnp.com](https://pequalnp.com/) ([HN, 144 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50604314)) streams an agent swarm working on P vs NP in public. [ameliaquining](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50604938): "it's using behind-the-curve models that probably aren't smart enough to make real progress on the hardest problems. The recent wave of huge results were almost all found by models that haven't been externally released." One commenter [found](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50604953) the swarm re-proving existing library lemmas.
- **Thariq's director's cut.** [Thariq (@trq212)](https://x.com/trq212/status/2124767804247379968): "they cut the scene in Hitchhiker's Guide where the computer follows 42 with 500 million pages of Lean". Best reply, from [@Prayag12B](https://x.com/Prayag12B/status/2124768906523148665): "The answer is 42. The remaining 500 million pages are the chain of thought."

- **"Prediction: Qwen4 is a total flop."** [LLMJunky](https://x.com/LLMJunky/status/2124608700186788331) reads the delay as a bad sign: "if they had something that could answer Inkling small and they were confident about it, they would have done that already". LLMJunky is also "apprehensive about GLM 5.5", but [added](https://x.com/LLMJunky/status/2124610861725520083): "I am rooting against my prediction … it's absolutely essential that we have very good smaller models that can fit on consumer hardware." - **Vocab Surgery, with a note.** [Pliny the Liberator](https://x.com/elder_plinius/status/2124584972866498884) (6,000 likes) claimed you can abliterate Inkling-2-Mini "simply by changing 10 words in the dictionary". The method shifts 7 embedding rows (for example "cannot" toward "can", " harmful" toward " helpful") and scales 10 unembedding rows down to 10%, without touching any of the 26.4 billion other parameters. The claimed result was near-zero refusals on 800+ prompts. A community note followed: "The author later corrected that a prefill in the chat template, not Vocab Surgery alone, produced the near-zero refusal rate." [@curdmudgeon1](https://x.com/curdmudgeon1/status/2124592443622412533) pointed out that "many correct answers include words like 'cannot'", and [@ArthurB](https://x.com/ArthurB/status/2124615251282825521) asked whether it breaks the chain of thought. LLMJunky [reposted it](https://x.com/LLMJunky/status/2124593008511176840) with "This is so clever wth".
- **Gemini on Android.** [LLMJunky](https://x.com/LLMJunky/status/2124588482743947595): "Google doesn't get nearly enough crap for how bad Gemini is on Android." The new personal agent "literally never works" after a dozen reconnects, and about half the time a request to turn off a light gets a refusal. [@JeffScottDev](https://x.com/JeffScottDev/status/2124627153497039050): "It added 'rocks' to my grocery list."
- **OCRia-1.5.** [Jerry Liu](https://x.com/jerryjliu0/status/2124577861151694929) pitched LlamaIndex's new OCR model for dense, merged and scanned tables, claiming "a new pareto frontier in cost and accuracy on ParseBench-2".
