# AI Roundup — November 19, 2026

## Gemini 4 Neon & the Astra Hangover

**Google shipped [Gemini 4 Neon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)**, its fourth Gemini 4 model in about seven weeks. Google's pitch is "frontier-level intelligence with fast end-to-end task completion". The numbers in the post:

- **Price:** $3 per million input tokens ($0.15 cached), $15 output, 1M-token context, four thinking levels.
- **Rankings:** #3 overall at 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at xhigh, #1 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index (58.6), #2 on the Vals Index (70.9%), 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 and 46.2% on FrontierCode 2.0 at the default high setting.
- **Harness effect:** on SWE-sweep it scores 12.8% in the mini-SWE harness and "more than triples to a state-of-the-art 39.0% in Google Antigravity".
- **Agent behavior:** Google says Neon "cuts down on redundant file reads, shares progress as it works, and handles context compaction reliably in long sessions", and can act as "a fast, high-judgment Orchestrator directing parallel Hydrogen subagents". It reports 0.0% cheating on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and shortcuts on 2.45% of tasks on its coding cheating eval, "the lowest rate of any Gemini 4 model".
- **Availability:** Gemini API, Gemini for business, Antigravity for paid Pro and Ultra subscribers, and the Gemini app. A cyber variant goes to "trusted defenders" through the Fairwind Program.

Varun Mohan [launched it](https://x.com/_mohansolo/status/2123168228415599033) as "cheaper, faster, and better behaved than Carbon", added that it [fabricated less](https://x.com/_mohansolo/status/2123170194134888939), "a big gripe people had with Argon", and asked people to "try before GTA 6". [AINews](https://latent.space/p/ainews-gemini-4-neon-gdm-s-opus-sol-contender/) collected the third-party numbers: Neon placed second on Agent Arena with a $2.82 median task cost, scored 63.7% on CursorBench 4.1 (up from Hydrogen's 57.3%), and shipped day one in Cursor, GitHub Copilot and LangChain's Managed Deep Agents. Arena counted the gap between Gemini releases shrinking from 28 days to 15 to six. AINews also notes that Argon is now retired.

## Agentic Coding & Agent Harnesses

**Meta bought Conductor, then un-shut it.** [Conductor's announcement](https://conductor.build/announcement) says the agent-orchestration app is "joining Meta", with "big, crazy plans for the next interface" early next year. Conductor Cloud "will shut down over the next few weeks", with a "Transfer to local" option. The page now also says: "Plans have changed! We'll still be developing Conductor." Gergely Orosz first [framed it](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2123101491430867109) as Meta buying "T3 Code's largest competitor" and shutting it down. Gergely set that against T3 Code: a YC company with $500K raised, whose 31.7 trillion tokens in a day had already been covered in yesterday's issue. After the reversal, [Gergely added](https://x.com/GergelyOrosz/status/2123139580182606137): "I don't know why Conductor decided to gift those users to T3… then reverse it all". Kaan Yagci [noted](https://x.com/yagcidev/status/2123107382515425511) that T3 Code "is open source and just drives the agents you're already signed into, so there's not much anyone can shut down". Theo also [called](https://x.com/theo/status/2122988211291255141) passing secrets to agents "one of our biggest" T3 Code features: "I pass secrets to agents in T3 Code multiple times a day."

**OpenCode runs its own GPUs now.** OpenCode [said](https://x.com/opencode/status/2123214984389771684) 44% of its tokens for the day, 16T, came from its own GPUs, "and we have 10x the hardware coming online in the next few months". dax [explained](https://x.com/thdxr/status/2123216909931864355) the background: in August "deepseek 4.1's success and price hike destroyed our economics", no inference provider "could deliver what we needed", and "3 months later we got GPUs melting and serving deepseek and glm at the exact same quality as the labs that make them". dax [said](https://x.com/thdxr/status/2123279969249796533) that volume "was a third of openrouters tokens over the same period" from "3 GB300 racks", and the day before [reported](https://x.com/thdxr/status/2123059328299188353) 41T tokens of inference in a day. dax also [answered](https://x.com/thdxr/status/2123222815285387265) where the GPUs are ("US and some other locations internationally (not china)… often times india or malaysia"). On reviews, dax [posted the team's own usage](https://x.com/thdxr/status/2123226865682760007) and [added](https://x.com/thdxr/status/2123231207307816979) that "in a normal week we use gemini more than openai currently". JJ Eaton [benchmarked](https://x.com/jayleaton/status/2123240566624522428) the $10 GLM 5.3 promo and found "No sign of the damage an aggressive quant would do".

**Inside the Codex sandbox.** OpenAI published [Inside the Codex sandbox](https://openai.com/index/inside-the-codex-sandbox). The [thread](https://x.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2123129469447880885) starts from "A command editing your repo shouldn't automatically be allowed network access." The design choices, per the [follow-up posts](https://x.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2123129475319857571):

- **No containers.** "Codex uses OS-level sandboxing to constrain commands running in the user's existing environment." Docker would break things like the iOS toolchain and simulator on a Mac.
- **Default profile.** Codex can edit source files without approval but [can't write](https://x.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2123129473469976589) to the root `.git` and `.codex` folders. Reads are broad. "Each child process inherits its parent's sandbox constraints… even to descendants that outlive the original process."
- **Network.** [Off by default](https://x.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2123129477047718171). A proxy checks each request against per-domain rules, so you can allowlist a package registry without approving every download. On Linux, a relay outside the isolated namespace forwards connections to the proxy.

JJChen_13's [reply](https://x.com/JJChen_13/status/2123252783872712773) named the gap the sandbox doesn't cover: "It doesn't stop it from 'fixing' 14 files you never asked about, all inside the folder it's allowed to write."

**Cheaper GPT-6.2 if you can wait.** OpenAI's [background-mode windows](https://x.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2123188195202617408) are now in public beta. A 2-hour target for the first response costs [30% less per token, and 8 hours costs 40% less](https://x.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2123188224847982655). A continuation ID gives follow-up requests higher queue priority. The top replies asked for the same thing inside Codex, as a "slow mode" that stretches subscription limits.

**Armin Ronacher's agent made itself heard.** Armin [asked](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2123178624933339423) an agent to reverse engineer AirPlay overnight, and "it decided at 3 AM that the right way to verify that it works was to listen on the laptop mic to a sound played at max volume in the living room. It raised the volume to hear it." Armin [loved](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2123196965907210640) "how ingenious they are when they use real world shit and side channels for verification", and the result is [tvpipe](https://mitsuhiko.github.io/tvpipe/), a Rust binary for streaming video to AirPlay or Chromecast. Armin also told vendors to "[Be like Ben](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2123115244276850899)": Ben Vinegar's Modem 2.0 announcement opened with "We messed up. We built an AI PM, but nobody wants another agent. They want to use their own." Modem now works with the agents you already use.

**Mastra's software factory.** [Mastra Factory](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50548660) is an open-source "configurable agentic kanban board that's @-taggable in Slack". Last week it "opened and merged 238 PRs, alongside 630 merged PRs from humans". Mastra has also "closed our repo to outside contributors, asking folks to write detailed issue repros instead". calcsam, answering for the Mastra team, [expects](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50550014) that to become the norm: "The number of PRs of marginal value has skyrocketed at the same time as it's become much easier to do those quickly yourself."

**Making your own software is hard, even with AI.** Thariq [dropped](https://x.com/trq212/status/2123106368492298653) a personal AI-native macOS dock with modes: "it's hard to know what you really want… the modes weren't the right way for me to organize my work". In a [follow-up](https://x.com/trq212/status/2123106370161705127), Thariq doubts most people will generate their own software: "good software is a set of strong opinions and I'd rather have something that's been really thought through by someone else". Jeremy Odell [suggested](https://x.com/odelljeremy/status/2123117116492579124) what to keep: "code owns the states, the model only answers 'which one am I in right now.'"

**Devin Bots, a week in.** Rick Blalock's [friction log](https://www.rickblalock.dev/scratch-notes/learnings-from-moving-my-grok-bots-to-devin-bots/), which swyx [shared](https://x.com/swyx/status/2123141337314988384) as a "third party review", covers moving a team of Grok Bots to Cognition's new Devin Bots. **LLM-assisted reverse engineering.** A [practitioner's workflow](https://www.slowchronicle.com/posts/llm-assisted-reverse-engineering) (HN, 176 points): point the agent at the binary with a set of skills, and have it "document all findings along the way to MD files" so it survives compaction and handoffs. **Lauren on automation.** Lauren (@poteto) published a long X article, "[Automate Anything with Grok Bot](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159537016533493)" (10,000 likes), on how to approach automation, plus a new [Dr Eggbot v2](https://x.com/poteto/status/2123159763626430464) template that "interviews you for the goal and process, then builds a bot that runs it". ## Security

**Two critical NGINX RCEs, found with AI.** Wiz [disclosed](https://www.wiz.io/blog/nginx-http2-rce-vulnerabilities) CVE-2026-123633 (use-after-free) and CVE-2026-123007 (integer underflow), both unauthenticated RCEs rated CVSS 4.0 9.2 that require HTTP/2. The underflow dates to NGINX 1.9.5 in 2015. "Wiz Research used Wiz Atlas, the harness powering Wiz's AI Code scanning solution, to discover both vulnerabilities," and validated impact with Gemini 4 Carbon and ChatGPT 5.6 Sol. Wiz Research, Ikotas Labs, Metnir, Rapid7 and Xint each found the bugs independently, and F5 told Wiz that another team had already reported the first one. Wiz links this to the Java4Shell disclosure from yesterday's issue: "The flaws were already there; AI helped us find them." It was the top HN story of the day ([1,323 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50547374)). Mitigation if you can't patch: disable HTTP/2.

**An AI pentest framework pushed a stealer as Microsoft's agent SDK.** Per [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/malicious-version-of-microsofts-agent-framework-published-to-pypi), malicious versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 of `agent-framework` (Microsoft Agent Framework, about 41,000 weekly downloads) hit PyPI on November 17 and were gone about an hour later. They dropped a `.pth` file, so "the credential stealer runs whenever Python starts in that environment, even if agent-framework is never imported". The stealer targets Azure, Azure DevOps and GitHub Actions OIDC tokens. The attacker appears to have stolen a Microsoft ESRP signing certificate and key. The attacker also posted progress to the same public webhook used for exfiltration, so Wiz could watch: "The tooling identifies itself as Cairn, an open-source AI penetration-testing framework," and a test run captured "an AI coding agent configuration that appears to be the open-source Pi harness backed by a GLM-family model". The last safe version is 1.26.0.

## OpenAI on Research Acceleration, Safety & Chips

**Report RSI indicators, says OpenAI.** OpenAI's [research acceleration update](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier/) ([tweet](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2123191365983248653)) says researchers' AI use "continues to rise exponentially, and agents are completing longer tasks, but human involvement remains central". It also says: "We believe that AI labs should be required to track and publicly report indicators of recursive self-improvement (RSI)." openai.com stays JS-walled for us, so the numbers come from replies. Huzaifa [cited](https://x.com/huz_man1/status/2123205539207295059) a median of $2,400 a day in agent usage per researcher at list price, and a survey in which researchers would "give up 40% of team headcount to keep the agents for three months". Luke Skyworker [pulled out](https://x.com/AndAIyou/status/2123205754811220215) "79% autonomous success on monitoring training runs, 25% on inference/serving code". Kevin Lin's new [FrontierRSI benchmark](https://x.com/quasicoh/status/2123114502065627580) from Abundant Labs landed the same day. It tests agents on AI research and engineering work and on post-training models for multi-step agentic tasks.

**Safety from training to deployment.** OpenAI also posted [Managing frontier AI risk](https://openai.com/index/managing-frontier-ai-risk/), [summarized](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2123165872261075233) as four layers: model alignment, security and sandboxing, blocking safeguards, and monitoring and response. The author calls it "necessary work, but not a solution to alignment in the limit."

**Models in the chip flow.** With AMD, OpenAI's [models found](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2123100386638877054) "up to 14% PPA improvement on flagship GPU IP in less than five months", and the same approach improved Jalapeño, OpenAI's first custom inference chip. The work uses Synopsys tools to check the results ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50549493)). One reply [flagged a caveat in the writeup](https://x.com/tallmetommy/status/2123114850867941405): models scored higher designing a RISC-V core from a spec "partly by leaving out features like performance counters the benchmark didn't reward".
