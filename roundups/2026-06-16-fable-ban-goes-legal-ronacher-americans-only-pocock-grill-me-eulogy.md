# AI Roundup — June 16, 2026

## The Fable Ban Goes Legal

**The "recklessness" accusation.** Four days after the Commerce Department ordered Anthropic to [suspend Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for all foreign nationals](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2065597531644743999), the political framing sharpened. [Fox Business reported](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/trump-admin-says-anthropics-recklessness-triggered-export-controls-latest-ai-models) that the Trump administration is calling Anthropic's handling of a reported jailbreak "reckless" — the Commerce Department moved after another company claimed it had jailbroken Mythos, and [David Sacks reportedly said Anthropic "refused" to fix the vulnerability](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/trump-adviser-david-sacks-says-anthropic-refused-to-fix-fable-5-jailbreak-before-us-export-controls) before the export-control letter landed. Anthropic's position: [the jailbreak "isn't serious"](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/trump-adviser-david-sacks-says-anthropic-refused-to-fix-fable-5-jailbreak-before-us-export-controls) and the order was disproportionate. The models remain offline for non-US users.

**The lawsuit.** The backdrop makes the ban harder to read as pure national security: Anthropic is [actively suing the administration](https://www.aol.com/articles/analysis-anthropic-strong-case-against-100446375.html) over a Pentagon "supply chain risk" designation it received after refusing to remove restrictions on military and surveillance use of Claude. [Time reported](https://time.com/article/2026/06/13/anthropic-fable-mythos-ban-US-security/) that a federal judge temporarily blocked the blacklisting while litigation continues, but the export-control order is a separate legal instrument — so Anthropic now sits on a Pentagon blacklist *and* under a Commerce Department licensing regime at the same time. Whether that's two agencies independently concluding Anthropic is dangerous, or one political apparatus retaliating from two angles, depends on who you ask.

**Anthropic's official statement.** ["We believe this is a misunderstanding and are working to restore access as soon as possible."](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) Fable 5 had launched June 9 with a [95% SWE-bench score](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/best-ai-coding-tools-june-2026-post-fable5) and was immediately the consensus best coding model — then pulled four days later.

## Ronacher's 'Americans Only' and the Sovereignty Question

**The essay that landed.** Armin Ronacher's ["Dangerous Technology For Americans Only"](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/6/13/americans-only/) (June 13) became the most-circulated European developer response to the Fable ban. His argument: the interesting thing isn't that Anthropic's own safety rhetoric backfired — it's the line the US government is drawing. "This technology is apparently so powerful that only Americans should have it." He pointed out that Anthropic's leadership spent years describing frontier AI as existentially dangerous and in need of strict controls, and the government simply took them at their word.

**The broader Ronacher week.** This was his third essay in six days — ["Gaslighting Openness"](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/6/10/gaslighting/) (June 10) argued that Open Source is being stressed by AI slop, shifting contributor dynamics, and large companies learning to close doors behind them, and ["Communities of Not"](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/6/6/communities-of-not/) (June 6) addressed the fracturing of developer communities. On Twitter, he continued fighting the [npm dependency-pinning battle](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2066173579507490840) and begging the timeline to [stop sending him "root cause confirmed" agent slop](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2066285039348850847).

## Pocock's /grill-me Eulogy and the /diagnose Loop

**The eulogy.** Matt Pocock's /grill-me skill — a four-sentence prompt that structures a Q&A session where the AI interrogates the user to extract and organize knowledge — [crossed into territory nobody planned for](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2064023481398824993). Someone used it to write a eulogy for their mother. Pocock's response: "/grill-me is the most impactful four sentences I've ever written." The skill's original purpose was developer knowledge extraction (learn a concept by having the AI grill you on it), but the structured-interview pattern turns out to work for any domain where you need to organize raw thoughts — including grief. The [mattpocock/skills repo](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills) sits at 80K+ stars.

**The /diagnose skill.** On the engineering side, Pocock has been working on [/diagnose](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/engineering/diagnose/SKILL.md) — a disciplined diagnosis loop for hard bugs and performance regressions: reproduce → minimize → hypothesize → instrument → fix → regression-test. Where /grill-me is about extracting knowledge from the human, /diagnose is about making the agent do methodical detective work instead of guessing. It's [now available in the skills repo](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills) alongside /triage, /tdd, and the rest.

**The /teach skill.** Also continuing to spread: Pocock's [/teach skill](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2064023481398824993), which he describes as ten years of teaching experience distilled into a prompt — it builds a personalized curriculum, generates exercises, and adapts to the learner. The skills framework keeps growing because each new prompt is just a markdown file, and the patterns transfer: grill, teach, diagnose, triage — they're all structured loops with clear exit conditions.

## Karpathy's Software 3.0 and the MenuGen Lesson

**The Sequoia Ascent fireside chat.** Karpathy's [fireside chat with Stephanie Zhan](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2049903821095354523) at [Sequoia Ascent 2026](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/sequoia-ascent-2026/) keeps generating discussion. The talk that's getting quoted most: **menugen**, a restaurant-menu app that photographs a menu and renders food images. The app became completely obsolete when Gemini could simply overlay images directly on the photo — no intermediate software needed. Karpathy's point: entire categories of apps are being "engulfed" by the model layer. Software 3.0 — where the program *is* the text in the context window and the LLM is the interpreter — collapses both traditional code (Software 1.0) and trained neural networks (Software 2.0) into prompting.

**"Never felt more behind."** Karpathy placed the inflection at December 2025, when his ratio inverted from writing ~80% of his own code to delegating ~80% to agents. His frame: "Vibe coding raised the floor; agentic engineering raises the ceiling." The professional discipline now is [coordinating fallible agents while preserving correctness, security, taste, and maintainability](https://analyticsdrift.com/andrej-karpathy-agentic-engineering-software-3/). The video is [on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=96jN2OCOfLs).

**The Anthropic move.** Context for why Karpathy's opinions carry extra weight right now: he [joined Anthropic's pre-training team in May](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/19/openai-co-founder-andrej-karpathy-joins-anthropics-pre-training-team/) under Nick Joseph, saying he thinks "the next few years at the frontier of LLMs will be especially formative."

## Tool & Ecosystem Updates

**Simon Willison: llm 0.32a3 and Datasette Agent.** Willison released [llm 0.32a3](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/9/llm/) on June 9 — the key new feature is human-in-the-loop tool calling via `ask_user()`, built specifically to support [Datasette Agent](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/10/datasette-agent/). The agent can now pause mid-chain to ask for human approval before executing destructive operations. He also posted [initial impressions of Claude Fable 5](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/9/claude-fable-5/) and shipped [Datasette 1.0a33](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/11/datasette/) with a custom extras API explorer built by Fable 5 and GPT-5.5. On June 15, a quiet one: [a Julia Evans quote](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/15/julia-evans/) about writing for a specific person — "often 'me, but 3 years ago.'"

**Thariq (trq212): Dynamic workflows and SpaceX compute.** Claude Code's [dynamic workflows](https://x.com/trq212/status/2061907538741006796) — where the agent writes its own harness on the fly, custom-built for each task — are what Thariq calls "the biggest upgrade to Claude Code's capabilities since skills and subagents." He dove into [best practices with @sidbid](https://x.com/trq212/status/2061907337154367865), highlighting non-technical tasks it enables. On the infrastructure side, he announced [doubled 5-hour limits and a SpaceX compute partnership](https://x.com/trq212/status/2052065936585457982) to scale capacity.

**Boris Cherny: The loops thesis.** The Claude Code lead's [WorkOS stage statement](https://x.com/laurenmhreeder/status/2051351487515902247) — "I don't prompt Claude anymore. I have loops running that prompt Claude and figure out what to do. My job is to write loops" — continued generating derivative content and debate. Combined with steipete's ["you should be designing loops that prompt your agents"](https://x.com/steipete/status/2063697162748260627) (5M+ views), the "loop engineering" framing is now the dominant paradigm in the agentic-coding discourse.

**Jerry Liu at Databricks.** The LlamaIndex CEO is [at Databricks Data+AI Summit this week](https://www.databricks.com/dataaisummit/speaker/jerry-liu) (June 15–18, SF), speaking on agents and RAG. His [Fusion API analysis yesterday](https://x.com/jerryjliu0/status/2066363868683866503) — arguing that the cost-accuracy Pareto curve belongs to *mixtures* of models, not frontier models alone — was one of the sharpest takes of the week.

**swyx: AI Engineer World's Fair countdown.** The [AI Engineer World's Fair 2026](https://www.ai.engineer/worldsfair/2026) is two weeks out (June 29 – July 2, SF). 29 tracks, 300 speakers, 6,000+ attendees. Boris Cherny [spoke at last year's event](https://x.com/bcherny/status/1932652135549972935) on the evolution of coding UX since the 1950s; this year the entire agenda will be shaped by the loop-engineering shift.
