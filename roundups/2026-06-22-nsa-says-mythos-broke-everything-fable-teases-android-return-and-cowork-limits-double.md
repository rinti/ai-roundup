# AI Roundup — June 22, 2026

## Fable 5 Export Ban: NSA Breach, Android Sighting & the Pricing Cliff

**The real reason behind the ban.** The Fable 5 / Mythos 5 export suspension entered its tenth day with the most significant disclosure yet. [NSA Director Gen. Joshua Rudd told a Senate Intelligence Committee briefing](https://bankwatch.ca/2026/06/21/nsa-chief-says-mythos-breached-almost-all-classified-systems-in-hours/) that Mythos, in a classified red-team exercise, **autonomously breached "almost all" of the NSA's classified systems within hours**. [Senator Mark Warner quoted the testimony publicly](https://digg.com/tech/mno1ygvv), and the implications are severe: this isn't the "jailbreak" described in earlier public accounts — it's evidence that Mythos's autonomous offensive cybersecurity capability is itself the national security concern, not a user-side exploit that can be patched. [Via TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318783/20260621/claude-fable-5-resurfaces-android-app-nsa-breach-testimony-reshapes-ban.htm).

**Fable 5 resurfaces in the Android app.** In the same window, developers noticed [Fable 5 reappearing in the Claude Android app's model selector](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318783/20260621/claude-fable-5-resurfaces-android-app-nsa-breach-testimony-reshapes-ban.htm). Where earlier attempts returned a hard "model unavailable" response, the new message reads "server is temporarily rate-limiting requests." [As of June 21, both models remain offline for every user on earth](https://explainx.ai/blog/is-fable-5-back-2026) — but the changed error message suggests infrastructure preparation for a return.

**The G7 thaw.** [Trump signalled softening after meeting Dario Amodei at the G7 summit in Évian-les-Bains](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-anthropic-trump-officials-deal-restore-fable-5-mythos-5/), calling him "nice" and "smart," though the [directive formally stands as of June 20](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318760/20260620/fable-5-ban-update-trump-softens-directive-stands-refund-deadline-closes-today.htm). The original [Anthropic statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) remains the only official company communication. The [Axios scoop from June 12](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/12/anthropic-trump-mythos-fable-national-security) first broke the suspension.

**The pricing cliff.** Today, June 22, is the last day of Fable 5's free-trial window. Starting June 23, usage would bill at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens — but Anthropic has not clarified how this transition works while the model is still suspended. [Via explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/when-will-fable-5-be-available-again-2026).

**Korea makes its bet anyway.** Amid the uncertainty, [South Korea signed the largest Claude Code enterprise deployment in Asia](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318811/20260621/korea-makes-largest-claude-code-bet-asia-nsa-testimony-extends-export-ban.htm), an interesting signal of international confidence despite the export turmoil.

## Claude Cowork Limits Doubled & Legal MCP Connectors

**The announcement.** [Boris Cherny](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2063028954546733462): "We doubled Claude Cowork usage limits for the next month. This applies to your 5-hr rate limits. If you've been saving up a big messy project, now's the time." The promotion runs [June 5 through July 5](https://mer.vin/2026/06/anthropic-doubles-claude-cowork-5-hour-limits-through-july-2026/) for Pro, Max, and Team plans. Weekly caps and quotas across other Claude products are unchanged. [Via The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/anthropic-claude-cowork-promotion/).

**What shipped alongside it.** [Anthropic also dropped 20+ legal MCP connectors and 12 practice-area plugins](https://actgsys.com/en/blog/anthropic-claude-cowork-limits-connectors-sme-2026-06), letting law firms and in-house legal teams work across research, contracts, discovery, and matter management inside Cowork. The strategy read, [per Pasquale Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/4328/anthropic-doubles-claude-cowork-limits-strategy): Anthropic is taking a loss on inference to seed habit formation in knowledge work before the billing changeover.

**Cherny on the bigger picture.** In a widely cited [interview with The Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/building-claude-code-with-boris-cherny), Cherny gave the cleanest definition of where he thinks things are headed: "I don't prompt Claude anymore. I have loops that are running. They're the ones that are prompting Claude and figuring out what to do. My job is to write loops." Claude Code now sits behind [close to 4% of all public GitHub commits](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2007179832300581177), and most new code at Anthropic is written through it.

## Loop Engineering Crystallizes Into a Discipline

**The post that started it all.** [Peter Steinberger (steipete)](https://x.com/steipete/status/2063697162748260627) — creator of OpenClaw, now at OpenAI — posted two sentences on June 8 that hit **6.5 million views**: "Here's your monthly reminder that you shouldn't be prompting coding agents anymore. You should be designing loops that prompt your agents." The AI-coding timeline spent the next week arguing, building, and naming.

**It's now a category.** In one week, loop engineering spawned: a [Latent Space feature ("Loopcraft: The Art of Stacking Loops")](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-loopcraft-the-art-of-stacking), an [open-source orange book on GitHub](https://github.com/alchaincyf/loop-engineering-orange-book), a [DEV Community guide](https://dev.to/max_quimby/loopcraft-stop-prompting-start-designing-loops-2lke), and [explainx.ai declaring it "the AI skill everyone is talking about"](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/loop-engineering-mainstream-ai-skill-june-2026). The core principles, [per the explainx.ai synthesis](https://explainx.ai/blog/loop-engineering-coding-agents-claude-code-guide-2026): anchor intent through files like VISION.md and AGENTS.md; provide guardrails (tests, type checks); offer reusable skills rather than one-off prompts; cap iterations; let it run on cron while the engineer decides what to build next.

**Theo capitulated.** As covered in [last week's roundup](https://x.com/theo/status/2067115748959682743) (161K views): "I hate to admit it but the loop people were right." The most useful reply came from [Travis Gautier](https://x.com/travisgautier/status/2067142240900620640): "Highly overrated for developing prod. Highly recommended for completing large batches of refactor targets or identified bugs."

## Dynamic Workflows: Claude Code Writes Its Own Harness

**Thariq's big feature.** [Thariq (trq212) announced dynamic workflows in Claude Code](https://x.com/trq212/status/2061907337154367865) on June 2, and the feature has been steadily reshaping how people use the tool. The [official Anthropic blog post](https://claude.com/blog/a-harness-for-every-task-dynamic-workflows-in-claude-code) explains: Claude can now write and orchestrate its own multi-agent harness on the fly — deciding how work gets split, which subagents spawn, what tools each gets, how output is verified, and when the job is done. The trigger word "ultracode" forces Claude Code to create a workflow. [Via InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/06/dynamic-workflows-claude-code/).

**Why it matters.** The default harness suffered from three failure modes: agentic laziness (stopping before finishing complex tasks), self-preferential bias (preferring its own results when verifying), and goal drift (gradual loss of fidelity across many turns). Dynamic workflows address all three by letting Claude build a task-specific harness with explicit verification steps.

**Staying in the loop.** [Thariq has been asking others at Anthropic](https://x.com/trq212/status/2061545633560010826) how they stay in the loop when agents run for 8-10 hours and cost real money. His recommendation: use "interview me" as a magic phrase — Claude switches into question mode before diving in. [Karpathy retweeted the thread](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2061928642029158853).

## Theo's Agent Autonomy: Overnight PRs & Sherlock's Discovery

**The overnight run.** [Theo's overnight agent experiment](https://finance.biggo.com/news/0630550c86453c18) remains the clearest proof-of-concept for loop engineering in practice: a single prompt at 2:29 AM on a Sunday produced **four stacked pull requests** on Lakebed, each autonomously reviewed, revised, and merged by 6:50 AM — zero human keystrokes. The agent constructed its own plan, dynamically generating a workflow that matched the dependency graph of the refactor rather than executing a pre-written pipeline.

**Sherlock discovers an undocumented API.** Even more striking: [Theo added a new feature to Lakebed](https://x.com/theo/status/2059397972073533710) without telling anyone, and woke up to find his agent Sherlock had already discovered it and shipped a new app on top of it — "perfectly to spec." The agent didn't need docs or announcements; it explored the API surface autonomously. [Lakebed is coming soon](https://x.com/theo/status/2062029750366089388) as an agent-native cloud platform.

**The caveat.** Theo's own assessment is measured: the overnight success was on a project he controls, with no external dependencies or real users. The thread eventually broke his SSH session. Tooling remains immature.

## Pocock's Skills v1.0 & the Power of Leitwörter

**Skills v1.0 shipped.** [Matt Pocock's skills repository](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/) — 135K+ GitHub stars, 11.7K+ forks — hit its first semver major with a [63% token-cost reduction via progressive disclosure](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/matt-pocock-typescript-skills-v1-progressive-disclosure-2026): load short summaries first, pull full skill bodies only when needed. The v1.0 update also added a formal user-invoked vs. model-invoked taxonomy and new routing skills for domain modeling and codebase design. [Via AIToolly](https://aitoolly.com/ai-news/article/2026-05-02-matt-pocock-unveils-skills-repository-engineering-insights-sourced-from-personal-claude-configuratio).

**Leitwörter: the words that steer the agent.** [Pocock named the technique behind his most effective skills](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2066922013000671731): a *leitwort* (from literary theory) is a repeated word or phrase an agent uses to guide its own behavior. "Zone of proximal development" in /teach, "tracer bullets" and "deep modules" in engineering skills — these phrases encode behavior in a concise token the agent reinforces by repeating. [He proved /teach works by solving a Rubik's cube on video](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2066874357612151271).

**The full AI coding workflow.** [Pocock's current setup](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2024874219662905676): Idea → /write-a-prd → PRD → /prd-to-issues → Kanban Board → ralph.sh → Ralph Loop → Manual QA. His [full walkthrough video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-QFHIoCo-Ko) shows the end-to-end process.

## Mitsuhiko: The Friction Is Your Judgment

**The talk.** [Armin Ronacher (mitsuhiko) and Cristina Poncela Cubeiro presented "The Friction Is Your Judgment" at AI Engineer Europe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_Zcw_sVF6hU), and it has become the go-to counterpoint to the "automate everything" crowd. The core argument: agent-generated code drifts toward the locally convenient answer — agents are optimized to produce code that runs and makes progress, often at the expense of quality. Friction isn't the enemy; it's where your judgment lives. Removing it lets agents ship garbage at scale. [Slides](https://mitsuhiko.github.io/talks/ai-engineer-talk/). [InfoWorld coverage](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4176534/ai-coding-agents-need-good-software-engineers.html).

**The ongoing critique.** Ronacher has been [pushing developers to experiment with local models](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2066922650438410363), praising [OpenRouter's model transparency](https://x.com/mitsuhiko/status/2067001312894468325), and [flagging the "slop" problem in GitHub and Twitter mentions](https://x.com/jeremyphoward/status/2036507393337729404) — a "massive degradation of code quality" that gets caught way too late. His argument: in an era of AI-accelerated development, the most valuable things in software — trust, community, quality — require sustained time and commitment.

The argument: an agent can't act on a doc it can't correctly read, and reading a real enterprise table is harder than it looks. His [Scaling Without Slop essay](https://www.latent.space/p/2026) for Latent Space argues the central challenge for 2026 is changing "the slope of slop" — increasing both quantity and quality.

- **Lauren (potetotes) open-sourced her /how skill.** [A skill that helps both humans and agents understand architecture](https://x.com/potetotes/status/2044142264298156289): "I often tell agents to 'use /how to understand how X works' which helps them fix root causes and not just symptoms."
