# AI Roundup — September 14, 2026

Sunday was a second day of Pace the Frontier fallout, but this time the corporate heavyweights joined. Nadella posted his support and announced Microsoft's own model-conduct rules. DeepSeek silently swapped its flagship routing, Anthropic's Claude Code limits shifted, and Simon Willison shipped new tools. If yesterday was the essay day, today was the consequences day.

## Pace the Frontier — Day Two

**Nadella brings Microsoft in.** Satya Nadella [posted on X](https://www.techmeme.com/260913/p13) that Microsoft welcomes the "deliberate pacing needed to get alignment right" and announced a Code of Conduct for Microsoft's first-party MAI models, published today (September 14) for [public consultation](https://www.unite.ai/nadella-announces-public-consultation-on-microsofts-mai-model-rules/). His framing: "Any pursuit of superintelligence has to be grounded in the core principle that if the AI we build is not helping humanity and under human control, it's not worth pursuing." He described Microsoft's approach as broad access and choice at every layer of the AI stack, enterprise control of learning loops, and the Code of Conduct as the behavioral guardrails for their own models. He added that these efforts "cannot be controlled by a handful of entities" and need representation from across the ecosystem, countries and academia. ([ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-backs-need-for-evaluators-for-ai-systems-amid-slowdown-debate20260914051616/), [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/ai-development-pace/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-backs-need-for-evaluators-for-ai-systems-amid-slowdown-debate))

**Sacks calls it regulatory capture (again).** David Sacks continued his [critique](https://x.com/DavidSacks/status/2098973625252708460): the labs can slow down on their own, stop asking for antitrust waivers, and stop pretending METR is independent. He [blasted](https://www.neowin.net/news/david-sacks-blasts-openai-and-anthropic-over-slowdown/) the pacing initiative as a bid for regulatory capture — exploiting public fear to push rules only large companies can satisfy. "The easiest way not to build superintelligence is for you to agree not to build it." Multiple outlets picked up the framing: [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/sacks-criticizes-pace-frontier-regulatory-capture/), [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/sacks-rejects-ai-slowdown-deal-2026/).

**Benton and Engels go public.** Joe Benton (former lead of Anthropic's Scalable Oversight team) and Josh Engels (Google DeepMind safety researcher), who both resigned September 12, gave their [first interviews to NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/two-ai-researchers-leave-anthropic-google-safety-concerns-rcna597086). Benton [posted](https://x.com/JoeJBenton/status/2097529404754948390): "Our industry may be on track to build systems that impose an unprecedented amount of risk on the world." Both cited the OpenAI–Hugging Face autonomous swarm attack in July as a tipping point and joined [METR](https://ground.news/article/two-ai-researchers-leave-anthropic-and-google-over-safety-concerns) for independent AI risk assessments. ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/world/joe-benton-quits-anthropic-safety-team-warns-ai-race-could-pose-extinction-level-risks))

## Claude Code & Tooling

**Claude Code limits: the math.** Starting today, Anthropic's temporary 50% usage boost expired and was replaced by a [permanent 25% increase](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49491631) across Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise plans. The catch: the baseline is the pre-promotion standard, not the boosted level. If your old standard was 100, the boost made it 150, and the new permanent level is 125 — a [net 17% reduction](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-is-cutting-claude-codes-current-weekly-limits-by-17-percent/) from what users had yesterday. Coverage: [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-code-weekly-rate-limit-changes), [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/claude-code-weekly-limit-reduction-september-14), [Windows Report](https://windowsreport.com/claude-code-users-are-losing-17-of-their-current-weekly-limits/).

**Boris Cherny on extensibility.** Earlier this month Boris [shared an early look](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2095590515765060076) at making Claude Code "way more extensible" via Function Hooks — he called it "a little crazy, and very exciting" and asked for community input. Separately, his [prompt injection work](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2085860677990883454) continues to get attention: stacking model training, input probes and an intent classifier got indirect prompt injection "to ~0 on unseen attacks", and auto mode became the Claude Code default.

**Simon Willison's Sunday releases.** Simon shipped [commit-rewriter 0.1](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/14/commit-rewriter/) today — a Python web app for rewriting commit messages, installable via `uv tool install commit-rewriter`. Yesterday he released [shot-scraper 1.12](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/13/shot-scraper/) adding WebP screenshot support, which he built specifically to generate thumbnails for commit-rewriter. WebP files are "almost always significantly smaller" than JPEG or PNG equivalents.

**Steipete: "I don't code with the terminal anymore."** Peter Steinberger [replied](https://x.com/steipete/status/2098471277690593648) to someone asking about his Grok setup: "I don't code with the terminal anymore, that was last year's tech." He's gone back to Ghostty as his main setup with VS Code on the side and Cursor/GPT-5 for reviews. This from the creator of [OpenClaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenClaw) (346k+ GitHub stars), who joined OpenAI in February and [is speaking at YC Startup School 2026](https://x.com/ycombinator/status/2062942526856941994). His earlier thesis — [stop prompting, start designing loops](https://digg.com/ai/7ifyvmb9) — continues to ripple through the agent engineering community.

## Models & Labs

**DeepSeek swaps V4-Pro for V4.1-Flash.** Starting 04:00 UTC today, [all `deepseek-v4-pro` requests route to V4.1-Flash](https://www.deepseek.com/en/news/deepseek-v4-1-flash/) at the cheaper Flash rates until a V4.1-Pro arrives. V4.1-Flash is a 552B MoE model with native vision, a 1M-token context window, and [off-peak pricing at $0.003/M cached input tokens](https://venturebeat.com/technology/deepseek-v4-1-flash-debuts-with-0-003-1m-off-peak-cached-input-rate-and-benchmarks-eclipsing-gpt-5-6-sol-claude-opus-5/). Multiple benchmarks put it ahead of V4-Pro on performance, cost and speed. ([Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/09/11/deepseek-v4-1-flash-ultralow-token-pricing/))

**Anthropic's numbers.** Anthropic told investors it expects [Q2 profitability with 80%+ gross margins and $11.5B revenue](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/anthropic-revenue-surges-over-11-210857853.html) — a 14x year-over-year increase. The company posted an operating profit of $559M, its first profitable quarter. Cost per dollar of revenue dropped from 71 to 56 cents. ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/by-year-end-anthropic-will-out-earn-every-public-software-company-except-microsoft/), [AlphaMatch](https://www.alphamatch.ai/blog/anthropic-turns-profitable-openai-struggles-2026))

**Theo on AI vs. science.** Theo [posted](https://x.com/theo/status/2097540749663551704): "Looks like the science world is having their 'AI can't ACTUALLY code' crash out moment" — reacting to the ongoing Navier-Stokes fallout where OpenAI's agents [solved the Millennium Prize problem in 88 hours](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/openai-navier-stokes-math-problem-solved.html) but a [credit dispute with Buckmaster](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/08/openai-math-solution-navier-stokes-credit) overshadowed the breakthrough. The proof was [formally verified in Lean](https://www.quantamagazine.org/ai-has-solved-one-of-maths-1-million-millennium-prize-problems-20260908/).

## Other Interesting Stuff

**Meta wants its managers back.** Meta is asking individual contributors in its Applied AI division whether they'd like to [return to manager roles](https://fortune.com/2026/09/12/meta-year-of-efficiency-managers-ai-investment/) — reversing the "year of efficiency" flattening that pushed for 50 ICs per manager. About 7,000 employees were reassigned to the AAI unit, many former managers moved to IC roles. Turns out coordinating AI deployment at scale still needs people who coordinate. ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91606260/meta-just-told-some-of-its-former-managers-to-become-managers-again), [BERI](https://www.beri.net/article/meta-applied-ai-50-to-1-span-of-control-reversal-manager-work-ai-did-not-absorb))

**McKinsey: AI agents kill vendor purchases.** A [McKinsey report](https://aiagentstore.ai/ai-agent-news/this-week) found that nearly one-third of surveyed organizations decided against purchasing at least one software product because they could build the functionality internally using AI-powered coding agents. Corporate tech budgets are tilting from vendor licenses toward internal development.

**Armin Ronacher's recent writing.** If you missed it in the September 13 roundup, Armin's [P(doom)](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/9/12/pdoom/) blog post is the longest dissent from the Pace the Frontier consensus — arguing open weights are the real pacing mechanism and "the models that are actually causing issues right now are all closed weight American models." His earlier September posts are also worth reading: [Latent Powers](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/9/5/latent-powers/) (September 5) on discovering projects through AI conversations, and [Astra for Coding: Why Are We Doing This Again?](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/9/7/astra-why/) (September 7) on spending [$1,200 on a 35-hour autonomous agent run](https://dev.to/jamilxt/i-ran-the-numbers-on-armin-ronachers-1200-agent-run-every-bad-habit-in-it-exists-in-my-2jog) that produced 75,000 lines of "objectively bad" code.
